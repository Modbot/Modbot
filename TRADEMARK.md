# Trademark Policy

**"Modbot"**, the Modbot logo, and the `modbot.co` domain are trademarks of the Modbot project's
copyright holder.

The [AGPL-3.0 licence](LICENSE) grants you extensive rights to the **software**. It grants you no
rights to the project's **name or branding**. These are separate things, and the licence says so
itself — copyright and trademark are distinct.

## You may

- **Say what your software is.** State accurately that your project is built on, derived from,
  compatible with, or a fork of Modbot. *"FooBot, based on Modbot"* is fine and welcome.
- **Redistribute official releases** unmodified, under the Modbot name.
- **Refer to Modbot** in documentation, articles, comparisons, tutorials and discussion. Nominative
  use needs no permission.
- **Run Modbot** for your community and call it Modbot, because it is.

## You may not, without written permission

- **Name a fork, service, or product "Modbot"**, or any name confusingly similar to it.
- **Use the Modbot logo** as the identity of a fork, a service, or a product that is not Modbot.
- **Register domains, app listings, or social accounts** using the name in a way that suggests you
  are the project.
- **Imply endorsement by, or affiliation with**, the Modbot project.

## If you fork

Please pick your own name.

This is not about control over the code — the AGPL already gives you the right to fork, modify and
run it, and that right is not affected by anything here. It is about **users being able to tell who
is responsible for the software they are running.**

A moderation tool holds a community's ban records, its members' presence history, and credentials to
a VRChat account with moderator powers. When something goes wrong — a data-handling mistake, a
security issue, a piece of behaviour someone objects to — people need to know whose decision it was
and who to raise it with. A fork carrying the same name makes that impossible, and the cost lands on
users rather than on either project.

The same reasoning applies in reverse: it protects you too. If Modbot upstream makes a decision you
disagree with, a distinct name is what stops it being attributed to you.

## Questions

If you want to use the name in a way this document does not clearly permit, ask. The answer is
often yes.
