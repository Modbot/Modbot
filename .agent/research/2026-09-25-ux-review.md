# UX review of the moderator web app

- **Date:** 2026-09-25
- **Covers:** `src/Modbot.Web` as it stands on `staging` (commit `030108ba`, plus the uncommitted
  changes to `Members.tsx` and `Requests.tsx`)
- **Read first:** `CLAUDE.md`, brand design, console look design, phone design, one view per person
  design, then every page, every popup and the shell.
- **Screenshots:** the capture helper wrote six files to `scratchpad/ux-screens/`, but they hold
  only two distinct images. `1440-members.png`, `1440-people.png` and `1440-team.png` are the same
  frame of the **Live** page at about 1050 px wide, taken from a running build that is not in demo
  mode and is older than the code: it says "Watching · Nobody watching", and nothing in the current
  `Live.tsx` writes that. `1440-live-loading.png`, `1440-requests-error.png` and `1440-vrchat.png`
  are one file that does not open as a picture. No phone or VR captures arrived. Everything below
  therefore comes from reading the code. The screenshot is cited only for things it shows that have
  not changed since (the sidebar). The screenshots in `assets/` are from 2026-09-16, which is before
  the Console look, and they are covered in finding 25.

The three people this review is for:

- **The 00:40 moderator.** On a phone, standing in an instance, with a Discord report open. Has
  about 15 seconds to learn who this is and what to do.
- **The head moderator in a headset.** Modbot is on a panel beside the game for four hours. They
  need to notice when something changes; they are not going to study a page.
- **The owner.** Deciding whether to trust Modbot with ban records and a VRChat login. Polish and
  hype read as a red flag, and so does anything that looks unfinished or inconsistent.

---

## Verdict

Modbot is very good at being honest. Every list says how old it is. A failed read is kept apart
from an empty one. "You may not be told" is never shown as "there is none". The audit log turns raw
events into sentences you can click. Kick and ban cannot fire twice. The phone layout never drops a
column. None of that is in dispute.

The trouble is that the app is organised around its data sources, not around the questions a
moderator asks. Here are the three biggest problems, ordered by how much they hurt the 00:40
moderator:

1. **The person popup does not answer "have we dealt with them before".** Is this person banned, a
   repeat offender, flagged, or the subject of notes? The answers are spread across the bottom of
   the left column, a panel in the Overview tab and three other tabs. The flags are not in the popup
   at all. On a phone the ban line comes after three blocks of identity.
2. **There is no "now".** The app opens on the member list, sorted by newest joiner. Bans and Flags
   are the 9th and 10th entries in the sidebar, below Calendar, Giveaways and Chat. The only waiting
   count anywhere is on Reviews, which is the least urgent queue in the app.
3. **The Console look draws everything at one volume.** Each panel, strip, badge and status square
   has the same weight. A "Banned" badge is the same 12 px tinted chip as a "Member" badge. The
   worst trust rank, Nuisance, is drawn more quietly than an ordinary "User". Nothing on a screen
   tells you what matters most.

---

## Findings, ranked by impact

### 1. The person popup buries the answer to "have we dealt with them before"

**Who:** the 00:40 moderator, most of all on a phone.

**Evidence.** In the left column of `components/subject/PersonPopup.tsx`, lines 164-201, the
order is:

- the profile (banner, picture, name, badges, a line about how fresh it is)
- the Discord card, with an **Unlink** button
- the Modbot account card
- **Membership**, which is where the only statement of ban status lives (lines 538-549: one line of
  `text-destructive`, "On the ban list since…") and where Kick and Ban sit (line 562)

The rest of the answer is elsewhere:

- The repeat-offender counts are the "History" panel inside the Overview tab (line 311).
- Notes are a separate tab (line 147).
- Case files are a separate tab (line 149).
- AutoMod flags appear nowhere in the popup. A search of `components/subject/` finds no flag at
  all, even though Live and People both know about them.
- `ProfileHeader` carries no standing of any kind.

On a phone `PopupFrame` stacks everything into one column (`subject/shared.tsx:212`). The ban line
comes after the banner, the bio line, the freshness line and the Discord card. The repeat count
comes after the whole left column.

**Why it got here.** One view per person design §4 organises the popup by account: VRChat on the
left, Discord in its own card, the Modbot account in its own card, and one tab per kind of record.
That is a sound way to store the data and the wrong way to present it. The spec's own opening
question, *"who is this person, have we banned them before"* (phone design §1), has no single place
on the screen.

**Instead.** Put a standing bar directly under the popup's title, above the fold at every width.
It is built from data the popup already loads:

```
Person  ·  Ada Lovebug                                  [×]
usr_4f1c…
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ ■ BANNED since Sep 3   ■ Repeat: 4 actions, 3 mods        │
│ □ 2 notes   □ 1 open flag   □ Case file (1)   □ Member     │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- Each item is a chip that opens its tab.
- Banned and Repeat are filled chips in the destructive colour. Notes and flags are outlined.
  "Clean" is one muted word: "No history".
- On a phone, Kick, Ban and Note go in a bar fixed to the foot of the popup (it replaces the app's
  bottom bar while a person is open). They are never at the end of a scroll.
- The Membership card keeps its details. It simply stops being the only place the verdict is
  written.

### 2. Banning someone from a phone and writing it up takes about 14 taps and loses the case file on the way

**Who:** the 00:40 moderator.

**Evidence.** Here is the traced path.

1. Tap Search, type the name, tap the result (2 taps plus typing).
2. Scroll the popup's left column past the profile, the Discord card and the account card to
   Membership.
3. Tap **Ban**. Pick reasons, type a note into a 3-row box, tap **Ban** (3 taps).
4. The dialog now says "Banned." with a **Close** button (`moderation/ModerationActions.tsx:230-240`).

The server has already written a case file and returned its id: `ModerationActionResult.caseId`,
`lib/api.ts:1139`. The dialog ignores it.

5. To attach the screenshot: close the popup, tap Menu, tap Bans, search again, tap "Open the case
   file" (`CaseFileCell.tsx:36`), scroll to Evidence, tap "Attach a screenshot or video"
   (`EvidenceGallery.tsx:243-246`), then pick the file.

That is about 14 taps and two searches for the same person.

Two more problems along the way:

- The note typed in the ban dialog and the case file's "What happened" are the same text entered in
  two places with two different labels.
- The popup's Cases tab opens a case file with `go('/cases/…')` (`SubjectCaseFiles.tsx:59`). That
  throws away the popup and lands on a page whose only way out is "Back to bans".

**Why it got here.** The ban dialog was designed before case files existed (M4), and the case file
was given its own page for linking (`App.tsx:112-120`). Nobody joined the two up.

**Instead.** Make the ban dialog the write-up.

```
Ban Ada Lovebug from the group?
[Harassment] [Crashing] [Other]           ← reason chips
What happened  ______________________     ← this IS the case file text
[＋ Add screenshot]  IMG_2231.png ✓        ← file input, camera roll on a phone
                              [Cancel] [Ban]
─ after ─
Banned. Case file written.
[Add another screenshot]  [Open the case file]  [Done]
```

The uploads start when the file is picked and are committed against the case file once the ban
returns. That makes 6 taps from the person to a documented ban. The Cases tab should open the case
file inside the popup stack, as `?subject=…&case=…`, rather than replacing the page.

### 3. There is no "now". The app opens on the member list, and the sidebar puts Bans ninth

**Who:** the 00:40 moderator, and the head moderator.

**Evidence.**

- `App.tsx:87` maps `members` to `/`, and `pageFor` falls back to Members for any address it does
  not know.
- The member list's default sort is "Newest joiner first" (`Members.tsx:63`).
- The sidebar order (`lib/nav.ts:15-66`) is Members, Requests, Discord members, People, Live,
  Calendar, Giveaways, Chat, **Bans**, **Flags**, Audit log. Calendar, Giveaways and an AI chat
  all come before the two pages a moderator on shift needs.
- Nothing anywhere answers "what changed since I last looked". The audit log shows new rows only
  while you are on it (`AuditLog.tsx:177-192`). The one "while you were away" surface is
  `WaitingAlertsBanner`, and that only covers critical alerts that reached nobody.

**Why it got here.** Analytics spec 10.1 argued for "one page per question" and against a
dashboard. That argument was about charts, and it was right about charts. It was then applied to
the whole app, so the app has no page for the question every shift starts with.

**Instead.** Add a **Now** page at `/`. Members moves to `/members`, and existing links keep
working through `MOVED`. Top to bottom:

```
Now                                            ■ All systems working
┌ Needs a decision ────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 3 join requests · 2 open flags · 1 review                    │
│  Ada Lovebug   flag: slur in #general  2m   [Open] [Dismiss] │
│  …                                                           │
├ In the group's instances ────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Murder 4  12/24   ■ 1 flagged here  ■ 1 banned-before here    │
├ Since you last looked (Sep 25 22:10) ────────────────────────┤
│ 2 bans · 1 case file · 14 joins · Mira changed a ban reason  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Reorder the sidebar by how often each page is needed on shift (finding 12).

### 4. The only urgency signal in the app belongs to the least urgent queue

**Who:** the head moderator and the 00:40 moderator.

**Evidence.**

- The sidebar has exactly one count: open reviews (`App.tsx:327-349`, `Chrome.tsx:103-111`).
  Reviews are checks on a moderator's own pattern of actions, and they can wait a day.
- Open flags are already counted by the server (`Flags.tsx:47`, `setOpen(r.open)`), but that count
  is shown only on the Flags page itself.
- Join requests have no count anywhere. `Requests.tsx` reads VRChat only when the page is opened or
  Refresh is pressed.
- The browser tab title never changes, so a moderator whose Modbot tab is in the background learns
  nothing.

**Why it got here.** `nav.ts:7-11` removed counts from the sidebar because a hard-coded prototype
number could be mistaken for a real one. That was the right call at the time. Reviews got a real
count back and the others never did.

**Instead.**

- Show counts on **Flags** and **Requests**. Requests can be read on a slow timer, or its count can
  come from the audit log's join-request events, so no extra VRChat request is spent.
- Show two tones: a solid destructive count when something is waiting on a person in the instance
  right now (a flagged person present on Live), and the current primary count otherwise.
- Put the total in the tab title, e.g. `(3) Modbot`.

### 5. The Console look has no way to say "this matters most"

**Who:** all three people. The head moderator suffers most, because they read in glances.

**Evidence.** The look gives every surface the same volume:

- Every panel is `bg-card`, and every strip is `bg-strip`.
- Strip against card is **1.08:1** in light mode. Page against card is **1.06:1** in dark mode.
  (Both are from the brand tokens, worked out with the WCAG formula.)
- The status mark is a 6 px square (`size-1.5`, `StatusRows.tsx:67`).
- Every `Badge` is 12 px text on a 10% tint of its tone (`ui/badge.tsx:15-21`).
- Every `Stat` value is the same size.

On the Health page, "Needs you" for VRChat is drawn in the same frame as "Database: online". The
same flatness shows in the popup (finding 1).

**Why it got here.** Console spec §1 set out to remove "template polish": shadows, gaps, uppercase
labels over big numbers. It succeeded. It also removed the only tools the old look had for
emphasis, and replaced them with nothing. The spec's rule is "no decoration"
(`console-look §1.2`), but emphasis is not decoration.

**Instead.** Add a three-step emphasis ladder to the spec, built from existing tokens only.

| Step | Where | How |
|---|---|---|
| Alarm | at most one per screen: "Banned", "Needs you", a flagged person in your instance | solid tone fill, white text, and a 3 px left rule on its panel |
| Notice | warnings, "stale", open counts | today's tinted strip (`bg-warn/10`) plus a filled square at `size-2.5` |
| Quiet | everything else | as now |

Status squares go from 6 px to 10 px. A panel that holds an alarm gets its strip tinted, not just
its text.

### 6. The worst trust rank is drawn more quietly than any other

**Who:** the 00:40 moderator.

**Evidence.**

- `ProfileBadges.tsx:36-38` removes the `warn` "Nuisance" pill whenever the rank is already
  Nuisance, so that the same fact is not drawn twice.
- What is left is `TrustRankBadge`: an outline badge in muted grey text with a 6 px square in
  VRChat's `#782F2F` (`TrustRankBadge.tsx:17-18`, `lib/trustRank.ts:27`). On the dark card that
  square is about **1.9:1**.
- On Members, Bans, Requests, People and Live, the rank badge is the only place Nuisance appears.

So the one rank that means "VRChat itself thinks this account is trouble" looks exactly like
"User", with a darker dot. The spec says `warn` is Nuisance (§11.2), and the code draws it the
other way round.

**Instead.** Draw `TrustRankBadge` in the `warn` variant for Nuisance, the `info` variant for
VRChat Team, and outline for everything else. Keep the VRChat colour square for all of them.

### 7. On a phone, the "Actions" button opens a keyboard cheat sheet

**Who:** the 00:40 moderator.

**Evidence.**

- The third button of the phone's bottom bar (`Chrome.tsx:365`) opens `ShortcutSheet` with the
  title "Actions".
- The sheet lists whatever keys the page has registered. For a list page that is "Next row",
  "Previous row", "Open the selected row" (`lib/listSelection.ts:40-60`), "Add a filter",
  "Search", "Search and commands" and "Keyboard shortcuts".
- Only six files register any keys at all, so on most pages the sheet holds just the two general
  rows.
- While a person popup is open, the one moment a phone user needs actions, the sheet has nothing
  about the person, because there are no popup keys.

**Why it got here.** Phone design §6 reasoned that every key already has a label, so the key list
could double as the list of actions. It is a clever reuse, but keyboard mechanics are not
moderation actions.

**Instead.** Make the third button context-driven:

- **In a person:** Ban, Kick, Add note, Open case file.
- **On Live:** "People flagged here", "Refresh".
- **On a list:** Filter, Sort.
- **Elsewhere:** replace it with **Now** (finding 3), which is the better use of the thumb.

Keep the keyboard sheet on `?` only.

### 8. The most permanent small actions have no confirmation and no undo

**Who:** the 00:40 moderator. On a phone, one mis-tap does it.

**Evidence.**

- **Dismiss on a flag** is an outline button sitting next to "Open a review", and it fires
  immediately (`Flags.tsx:263-272`). The page's own comment says "a dismissal is permanent for that
  rule and that person" (`Flags.tsx:24`).
- **Unlink** a Discord account is one outline button with no confirmation
  (`DiscordLinkCard.tsx:53-61, 101`). It sits in the popup's left column, directly above Membership
  and its Ban button.
- **Take back** on a note is immediate (`PersonNotes.tsx:97`).
- By contrast, Kick, which can be undone by the person simply rejoining, gets a full dialog.

Spec §12.1 claims each destructive action's weight "matches how much the action takes away". These
three contradict it.

**Instead.** Adopt the rule "every action that removes something is confirmed or can be undone,
never neither". For dismiss, unlink and take back, act at once and show an "Undo" strip for 10
seconds, with the server holding the change until then or reversing it. Move Unlink behind the
Discord card's own menu so it is not beside Ban.

### 9. The member list, which is the home page, cannot show trouble

**Who:** the 00:40 moderator.

**Evidence.**

- Members has columns for Person, Discord, Roles, Joined, Last seen and actions
  (`Members.tsx:255-265`). It has nothing for banned, flagged, repeat or notes.
- People has a Standing column (`People.tsx:316-326`), and Live shows standing and flags
  (`Live.tsx:260-265`). A moderator scanning the member list for trouble cannot see any.
- What you can tap differs by page:
  - On People and Discord members the whole row opens the person (`People.tsx:248`,
    `DiscordMembers.tsx:231`).
  - On Members, Bans and Requests only the name opens it. On a phone the name is a line of text
    about 18 px high inside a 46 px row.

**Instead.**

- Every list of people gets the same Standing cell: Banned, Repeat, Flagged or Notes, as the chips
  from finding 1 at half size.
- Every row opens its person when tapped anywhere that is not a button.
- Add a Standing filter to Members.

### 10. A popup over the page is right for a quick look and wrong for working

**Who:** the 00:40 moderator (phone) and the head moderator (desk and headset).

**Evidence.**

- From `md` up, the popup covers the whole window less 1 rem, up to 100 rem wide
  (`subject/shared.tsx:204-207`). The list behind it is dimmed and blocked.
- `useModal()` switches off the page's keys while the popup is open (`SubjectPopup.tsx:54`). So
  `j`/`k` cannot move to the next person, and two people can never be compared side by side.
- On a phone the popup is the whole screen, but it is still a dialog. The browser's back button
  closes it (good), yet it has no address of its own that reads as a page, and its only way out is
  a 44 px × in the top corner, far from the thumb.

**Why it got here.** Foundation §10.2 wanted "look without losing the scan" and first specified a
side pane. `SubjectPopup.tsx:20-23` turned the pane into a centre popup because identity plus tabs
would not fit in a narrow pane. The popup keeps the scroll position underneath, but it hides the
scan, which is the thing that was meant to be kept.

**Instead.** Use a different shape at each width, with one address scheme (`?subject=` unchanged):

- **Wide (≥ 1280 px):** a split view. The list keeps the left 40%, and the person opens in a pane
  on the right: identity and standing bar on top, tabs below. `j`/`k` keep moving the list, and the
  pane follows.
- **Medium:** today's popup.
- **Phone:** a real page (`/people/<id>`, reached from `?subject=` with a redirect), with the
  action bar from finding 1 and a Back control at the foot.

A world or instance opened from a person replaces the pane content and keeps the "Back to …" link.

### 11. Ten tabs in the person popup, and the history is not a timeline

**Who:** the 00:40 moderator and the head moderator.

**Evidence.**

- The person popup can show Overview, Logs, Notes, History, Cases, Discord, Messages, Account,
  Metrics and JSON (`PersonPopup.tsx:144-155`). That is ten tabs in one sideways-scrolling row
  (console spec §11.5). In VR, sideways scrolling with a laser pointer is miserable.
- "Latest" on Overview merges 8 facts from five reads (`PersonPopup.tsx:241-281`). Joins, leaves
  and avatar changes crowd out the one ban. Nothing filters to moderation only, and nothing marks a
  ban as bigger than a join.
- Notes and case files, the most deliberate records the group has, live in separate tabs.

**Instead.** Use four tabs.

- **Timeline:** everything, newest first. Moderation events (bans, kicks, notes, case files,
  flags) are full rows. Presence (joins, leaves, avatar changes) collapses into runs such as "12
  visits between Sep 10 and Sep 22". A switch at the top chooses "Moderation only" or "Everything".
- **Profile changes:** today's History tab.
- **Discord:** Discord history and messages together.
- **Details:** Metrics, Account and JSON, stacked.

Put the note box at the top of Timeline, so writing a note happens where notes are read.

### 12. The sidebar is a table of contents, not a map of the work

**Who:** everyone, and the new moderator most.

**Evidence.**

- There are 20 entries (`lib/nav.ts`). The first 11 have no group heading at all.
- The screenshot `1440-members.png` shows the label **Team** twice, once as an Analytics page
  ("Team", `g t`) and again as a group heading over Reviews.
- The Modbot status rows are pinned to the sidebar's foot with `mt-auto` (`StatusRows.tsx:58`).
  With a group banner and 20 entries they fall below the fold at 900 px tall. The same screenshot
  shows only "VRChat · not configured" at the bottom edge, with a scrollbar on the rail.
- Keyboard hints (`G M`, `G J`…) sit on every row in faded text (see finding 21).

Here are a moderator's likely top five jobs and what each costs today:

| Job | Today | With the proposal |
|---|---|---|
| Who is this person? | Ctrl K and type, or 2 taps and typing on a phone. Good. | same |
| Are they banned, a repeat offender, flagged? | open them, then scroll and switch tabs (finding 1) | 0 extra: the standing bar |
| Ban and document | about 14 taps (finding 2) | 6 |
| Answer join requests | Menu, Requests, row, confirm. Nothing tells you any are waiting. | badge, plus on Now |
| Deal with a flag | Menu, Flags, name, popup, scroll, Ban | a Ban button on the flag row |

**Instead.** Group the pages by the work, and keep the status visible:

```
[Group banner]
Search            Ctrl K
Now            3
Live
Queue          5   (Requests · Flags · Reviews, as tabs)
People             (one directory, see finding 13)
Bans and case files
Audit log
── Numbers ──   Team · VRChat · Discord   (Worlds, Instances inside VRChat)
── Plans ──     Calendar · Giveaways
── Modbot ──    Health · Logs · Settings
Chat               (move to the palette and the popup, see finding 13)
────────────
■ All working       ← one line, always visible; expands on hover or tap
```

### 13. Plain words have produced labels that are vaguer than the jargon they replaced

**Who:** the new moderator.

**Evidence.**

- **Three lists of people.** "Members", "Discord members" and "People" sit next to each other, and
  "Users" and "Roles" are one level further down in Settings. Nothing on screen says that People is
  "everyone Modbot has ever seen" (`nav.ts:23-26`). A new moderator will try Members, fail to find
  a visitor, and give up.
- **Two things called History in the same popup.** There is a panel called "History" on the
  Overview tab (acted-on counts, `SubjectHistory.tsx:45`) and a tab called "History" (profile
  versions, `PersonPopup.tsx:148`).
- **Three things called Logs.** The popup's "Logs" tab, the sidebar's "Logs" page (Modbot's own
  program log) and "Audit log" are three different things.
- **"Requests" alone.** Requests to do what?
- **Two names for the density setting.** The density bank says "Comfy" (`Chrome.tsx:236`), and the
  palette says "Comfortable density" (`App.tsx:376`).

**Why it got here.** The naming rule tests single words ("would a volunteer understand
*dossier*?"). It does not test a word against its neighbours. Each label passes alone and fails
beside the others.

**Instead.**

- Make one **People** page with a scope switch: `In the group · On Discord · Everyone seen`.
  Members and Discord members become two of its scopes.
- Rename:
  - popup "History" tab → **Profile changes**
  - Overview "History" panel → **Past actions**
  - sidebar "Logs" → **Modbot's log**
  - popup "Logs" → **Timeline**
  - "Requests" → **Join requests**
  - "Comfy" → **Comfortable**
- Add to the naming rule: "no two labels a moderator can see at once may share a word for
  different things" (see Rules).

### 14. Loading, empty and failed look almost the same, a failure offers no retry, and some failures vanish

**Who:** all three people. For the owner this reads as "unreliable".

**Evidence.**

- The only differences between states are an 8 px square, hollow or filled red, and the grey or
  foreground colour of the words (`PanelGrid.tsx:39-72`). "Loading…" and "Nobody matches" are
  drawn identically.
- No failed state anywhere has a "Try again" control. The user has to reload the whole app.
- Some failures are silent:
  - `SubjectCaseFiles` returns `null` on error (`SubjectCaseFiles.tsx:43`), so the Cases section
    simply is not there.
  - `NotesBeforeActing` draws nothing when the read fails (`PersonNotes.tsx:178-188`). At the
    moment of a ban, "the notes failed to load" and "there are no notes" look the same, which is
    the exact trap the spec says it avoids.
  - A failed search in the palette reads as "Nothing matches" (`CommandPalette.tsx:88-90`).
- The brief asked about §22.2 of the console spec. §22 has only §22.1 (ages against the browser's
  clock). Nobody has written the loading and failure drift down.

**Instead.**

- **Loading** is a pulse in the shape of the rows that are coming (two or three grey bars, one row
  high).
- **Empty** stays as today.
- **Failed** is a `Notice tone="danger"` with the reason and a `Try again` button at `xs`, inside
  the panel.
- Nothing that can fail may draw `null`. The notes on a ban confirmation say "Could not read the
  notes" in warn tone.

### 15. "Never explain" leaves a first-time moderator with nothing to go on

**Who:** the new moderator, and the 00:40 moderator on a phone.

**Evidence.** Here are a new moderator's first ten minutes, traced:

1. The invite link opens "Create your Modbot account" (`Join.tsx:75`).
2. Then "Link your VRChat account" (`LinkVRChat.tsx:17`).
3. Then they land on the member list, sorted by newest joiner. No screen tells them:
   - that the Companion App exists or why they want it (it is what fills Live), or
   - where the ban button lives, or
   - that `Ctrl K` finds anyone.

Along the way:

- Live says "Nobody watching" (screenshot) or shows a grey "Last seen 08:53" list. Nothing tells
  them that *they* are the missing piece.
- Empty states state a fact and stop: "Nobody is waiting", "No flags", "No open instances".
- A disabled control gives its reason only in `title`, which a phone cannot show. Phone design §8
  admits this and files it under §9 because `CLAUDE.md` forbids visible explanation.

**Why it got here.** `CLAUDE.md` says: "Never add explanatory text to the UI unless someone
explicitly asks for it." The rule rightly killed paragraphs about *how Modbot was designed*. It also
killed the three short sentences a newcomer needs, *what can I do here next*, which are a different
kind of text.

**Instead.** Narrow the rule (see Rules), then:

- Put a first-run checklist on Now: "Pair the Companion App · Find someone with Ctrl K · Read the
  ban reasons". It is ticked from real data and disappears when done.
- Give each empty state one next action, as a button, not prose. Live with nobody watching gets
  **Pair the Companion App**. Flags with none open gets **See dismissed**.
- Every disabled control gets its reason as one muted line under it on touch screens.

### 16. VR mode is the desktop with bigger numbers, and nothing helps you notice a change

**Who:** the head moderator in a headset.

**Evidence.**

- The VR block in `index.css:157-184` raises heights, text and hairlines. The layout does not
  change:
  - The sidebar is still 13.5 rem, and at 18 px text its labels truncate.
  - The keyboard hints still show (`Chrome.tsx:115-123`, hidden only below `lg`).
  - The 18+ card opens on hover (`UserProfileCard.tsx:233-238`).
  - Tables and tab rows scroll sideways.
  - About 35 `title` tooltips carry details.
- For noticing change:
  - Live redraws silently when someone arrives (`Live.tsx:67-76`). A new arrival looks exactly
    like someone who has been there an hour, apart from a time in small grey text.
  - The audit log's "3 new" button appears only if you have scrolled.
  - Nothing flashes, nothing is marked "new", and nothing counts "since the event started".

**Why it got here.** The comment in `index.css:21-26` argues well for *sizes* in a headset. Nobody
argued for *layout* or for *time*.

**Instead.** Treat the headset as a place, not a density (finding 17), and give it a glance
layout:

- one column and no sidebar (the phone's bottom bar, bigger)
- Live as the default page
- arrivals in the last five minutes marked **New** for 60 seconds, then settling
- flagged and banned-before people pinned to the top of each instance
- a running line along the top: "Since 20:00: 212 arrivals · 3 kicks · 1 ban"
- no hover-only anything, and no sideways scroll: tables become stacked rows

### 17. Density is the wrong setting, and it holds the best spot in the top bar

**Who:** the head moderator and the owner.

**Evidence.**

### 21. Small text fails contrast, and several tap targets are only text-sized

**Who:** everyone. The head moderator in a headset, and the 00:40 moderator on a phone.

**Evidence** (worked out from the tokens with the WCAG formula):

- **Faded ids.** The id under a name is `text-muted-foreground/70` at `--text-tiny`, which is 11 px
  at dense (`Bans.tsx:231`, `People.tsx:284`, and 13 uses of `/60` and `/70` in all). That is
  **3.1:1** in light mode and **4.2:1** in dark, below the 4.5:1 AA needs at that size.
- **Sidebar keyboard hints.** They are `/60`, which is **2.5:1**.
- **The "Banned" badge.** It is `text-destructive` on its own 10% tint, which is **4.1:1** at 12 px.
  The most important badge in the app fails AA in light mode.
- **Small targets.** Tap targets below `lg`: a person's name inside a Members, Bans or Requests row
  (finding 9), and the "All logs" link on a popup strip (`subject/shared.tsx:112-123`), which is
  text-sized with no minimum height.
- **Motion.** The instance tile zooms on hover with no `motion-reduce` guard
  (`InstanceCards.tsx:89`). So does the audit row's turning chevron (`AuditLog.tsx:399`). The chevron
  in `EntryDetail.tsx:198` has the guard.
- **Accessible names.** Sign out is an icon-only button named only by `title`
  (`Chrome.tsx:212-214`). It needs an `aria-label`.

What is already right: status is never colour alone (squares are `aria-hidden` and words carry the
state), and dialogs name themselves.

**Instead.**

- Drop the `/60` and `/70` fades on text. Use full `text-muted-foreground`, and use size to step
  down, not opacity.
- Draw the destructive badge as solid fill with white text (it is an alarm, finding 5).
- Give the whole row as the target, and add `min-h-(--control-h)` to `More`.
- Add `motion-reduce:transform-none` to both transitions.

### 22. The new instance tile is right, and it breaks the look's rules without saying so

**Who:** the owner, who reads inconsistency as unfinished.

**Evidence.** `InstanceTile` (`InstanceCards.tsx:80-110`) and `InstanceBadges.tsx:13-14, 73-101`
break five of the rules:

- `rounded-md`, which console spec §19.1 forbids
- hex colours in a component (`#1a8fe3`, `#3ddc84`, and the flags), which brand spec §3 calls "a
  bug"
- `bg-black/70` and `text-white`
- `font-semibold`
- a hover zoom

Console spec §22 lists none of these.

It is also the best-looking, most recognisable thing in the app. A moderator matches it to the game
at a glance, which is exactly the job.

**Why it got here.** The rule "no colour that is not a token" had one exception, for trust rank
colours, which are "VRChat's data". The instance tile is the same kind of exception and nobody wrote
it down.

**Instead.** Add an "in-game likeness" clause to the spec (see Rules). Then use it on purpose. A
person row could carry the nameplate colour of their rank as a thin rule, which is how a moderator
recognises ranks in the game.

### 23. Times are written six different ways

**Who:** the owner, and anyone comparing two screens.

**Evidence.** 18 files format dates by hand instead of through `lib/format.ts` or
`charts/format.ts`:

- Health shows times with seconds and no options: `toLocaleTimeString()`, e.g. `3:41:07 PM`

## Rules I would change

**1. UI text (CLAUDE.md, "UI text — controls, not explanations").** The rule is right about essays
and wrong about next steps. Replace it with:

> **Controls, not essays.** A screen never explains how Modbot works, why it was designed that way,
> or what could go wrong in theory; that belongs in `.agent/specs/` and code comments. A screen
> *does* say, in one short line, what the person can do next when there is nothing else to do:
> an empty state may carry one action button; a disabled control shows its reason as one muted
> line on touch screens; a first-run checklist may appear until its items are done. If a screen
> needs more than one line to be understood, fix the screen.

**2. Emphasis (console look §1, new point 4, and a new §9.4).**

> **One loud thing.** Each screen may draw at most one thing at *alarm* weight — solid tone fill
> and white text, with a 3px rule on its panel — and it is always the thing a moderator must act on
> or know first (banned, needs you, a flagged person in your instance). *Notice* weight is the tinted
> strip and a 10px filled square. Everything else is quiet. Status squares are never smaller than
> 10px.

**3. Naming (CLAUDE.md, "Naming — plain words only", added at the end).**

> **One word, one thing, on every screen.** No two labels a person can see at once — in the
> sidebar, on one page, or in one popup — may use the same word for different things ("History",
> "Logs", "Team"). And the rule covers every screen: Settings, Health and setup are read by the
> owner, and "IAM", "producers", "probe" and "service account" fail the same test as "dossier".

**4. Destructive actions (console look §12.1, new first line).**

> Every action that removes, dismisses, unlinks or takes back something is either confirmed in a
> dialog or can be undone for ten seconds afterwards. Never neither. An action whose effect is
> permanent (a flag dismissal, a purge) is confirmed.

**5. Opening a person (one view per person §4.3, and foundation §10.2 as the app applies it).**

> On a wide screen a person opens in a pane beside the list, and the list keeps working. On a
> phone a person is a page with the actions fixed at its foot. The centre popup is for widths in
> between. The address (`?subject=`) is the same in all three.

**6. Density (index.css header, console look §19.2).**

> Where Modbot is used — **Desk** or **Headset**, with the phone detected — decides the layout.
> Dense and Comfortable are a desk preference, set in Your account, not in the top bar.

**7. In-game likeness (console look §1.1, beside the trust rank exception).**

> A thing that stands for something in VRChat — an instance, a trust rank, a platform, a region — may
> be drawn the way the game draws it, including the game's colours and its corner radius, inside a
> part that exists for that purpose (`InstanceTile`, `TrustRankBadge`, `InstanceBadges`). Nothing else
> borrows from those parts. Hover motion there honours `prefers-reduced-motion`.

**8. Screenshots (console look §20, "A new page", appended).**

> When a page's shape or the look changes, the README and docs screenshots of it are taken again
> from demo mode in the same change.

## Five ideas bigger than a fix

**1. A "Now" screen as the front door.** One page that answers "what needs me" and "what changed":

- the decisions waiting (join requests, open flags, reviews, Discord reports)
- each open instance with its flagged and banned-before people pulled to the top
- Modbot's health as one line
- "since you last looked", which needs a per-account last-seen time stored on the server

**Cost:**

- One new endpoint that aggregates what four pages already read.
- A last-looked time per account.
- The hard part is restraint: this page must never grow into the chart dashboard spec 10.1 got rid
  of. Numbers stay on the analytics pages. Now only lists things a person can act on.

**2. Person-first navigation.** Moderation is almost always "about this person". The search box
becomes the top of every screen, not a button. Beneath it sit **recent people** (the last ten you
opened, kept per account) and a **watchlist** (people the team pinned, e.g. "banned-before, known
alt"), whose members light up anywhere they appear: Live, the audit log, join requests.

**Cost:**

- Two small tables (recent, watchlist) and a live event when a watched person arrives.
- The watchlist is a moderation decision, so it needs its own fact and permission.
- It also answers the 00:40 question before the moderator asks it.

**3. One triage queue.** Join requests, flags, reviews and (later) reports from Discord are four
kinds of "someone must decide". Put them in one queue:

- **claim** a row, so two moderators don't both answer it
- **decide** with one key or one swipe (approve/reject, keep/dismiss, close with note)
- **move on** to the next row automatically

**Cost:**

- Each source already has its own decision endpoint, so the queue is mostly a client.
- Claiming needs a small server table and a live event.
- The real work is agreeing on the order: newest first, or "person in an instance right now"
  first. That is the order the moderator on shift needs.

**4. A mobile mode fed by the Companion App.** The Companion already knows who is in the
moderator's own instance and can capture clips (companion clips spec). The phone app opens on
**In your instance now**:

- everyone present, sorted with the riskiest first
- tapping a person shows the standing bar
- **Ban** attaches the Companion's last clip or screenshot to the case file automatically

This turns the 14-tap write-up into three taps, with evidence the moderator didn't have to capture.

**Cost:**

- A Companion-to-server "who is with me" view (most of it exists in presence reports).
- Clip hand-off from the Companion.
- Privacy wording on the evidence: an automatic capture must say it was automatic, and
  `origin: 'Captured'` already exists for that.

**5. An event mode for the head moderator in a headset.** Start an event and Modbot becomes a
glance board for its length:

- the event's instances as tiles
- a ticker of arrivals, kicks and bans since it started, with the newest marked for a minute
- a counter strip at the top
- a handover note box that the next head moderator sees when they take over
- at the end, a one-page event report (who came, what was done, by whom) that can be posted to
  Discord

**Cost:**

- An "event" record, which could simply be a calendar event marked active.
- A headset layout (finding 16).
- A report page.

It gives Calendar a reason to exist beside moderation instead of beside Giveaways.

## What I would leave alone

- **Facts as sentences** (`factSentence.tsx`), with every name clickable. This is the best thing in
  the app.
- **Honesty about age and absence:** `Freshness`, `Unread`, "not read yet", withheld against
  absent, "Counts rebuilt 4m ago". Keep all of it. The findings above ask for it to be louder, not
  softer.
- **Actions that cannot fire twice.** The key made when the dialog opens, and the dialog naming
  the person and the action.
- **Notes shown before acting**, as an idea. It needs a failure state (finding 14), not a redesign.
- **One app, nothing dropped on a phone**, with the first column pinned while a table scrolls.
- **The address carrying the popup stack**, so any view can be pasted to another moderator and back
  closes one level. Finding 10 changes the shape, not the addresses.
- **Tokens, the two themes, mono for ids and numbers, and no shadows or gradients.** Findings 5 and
  22 add emphasis and one exception. They do not bring decoration back.
- **The instance tile** (finding 22 asks to legalise it, not remove it).
- **The command palette** as the fastest way to a person on a desk.
