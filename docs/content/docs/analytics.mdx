title: Analytics
description: The five Analytics pages (Team, VRChat, Worlds, Instances and Discord), what each one shows, and where its numbers come from.
---

Analytics is a set of five pages, each answering one question about your community. They are in the
sidebar under **Analytics**, with **Worlds** and **Instances** under **VRChat**:

| Page | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| [**VRChat**](#vrchat) | Is the VRChat group growing or shrinking, and what changed? |
| [**Discord**](#discord) | Is the Discord server healthy, and who keeps it going? |
| [**Team**](#team) | Who is doing the moderation work, and when is nobody covering? |
| [**Worlds**](#worlds) | Which of our worlds actually get used? |
| [**Instances**](#instances) | When is the community actually active? |

All five need the **See analytics** permission
([roles and permissions](/accounts/roles-and-permissions#see-analytics)). The built-in **Moderator**
and **Viewer** roles have it. Without it, the pages are hidden from the sidebar and Modbot refuses
their data.

## Date ranges

Every page has the same range buttons at the top: **7 days**, **30 days** (the default), **90 days**
and **All time**. The dates the range covers are shown on the right.

- Ranges are whole days in UTC, and include today. Today's figures are still filling in.
- **All time** starts at the first day Modbot has anything recorded.
- Switching range keeps the old figures on screen until the new ones arrive.

A few panels ignore the range because they describe *now*, such as who is a member today. They are
noted below.

## Where the numbers come from

The pages draw on two kinds of stored data.

**Daily totals** are counts per day: joins, leaves, messages, bans, instances opened, actions per
moderator, and so on. Modbot works them out from its record of events about every 15 minutes, so a
chart can be up to about 15 minutes behind. Daily totals are **kept forever**, even if you set
Modbot to delete old events.

**The fact log** is Modbot's record of individual events (a join, a role change, a person seen in an
instance). Panels that need to pair events up, such as "did this invite lead to a join?" or "how
long was this instance open?", read the fact log directly. If you have set
[data retention](/self-hosting/data-retention), old events are deleted, and these panels can only
cover what is left.

### Data covered

At the bottom of every page, the **Data covered** card says:

- **Daily totals cover**: the first and last day with daily totals, and when they were **last
  updated**.
- **The fact log covers**: the first and last day with stored events.
- **Facts kept for**: how long moderation events and presence events are kept, or **forever**.

If a chart looks empty or short, check this card first. A deployment that started recording last
week has no figures for last month, and a short retention window shortens the fact log panels.

### Presence reports

Some figures (time spent in worlds, visitors, who was in an instance, coverage gaps) come from
**presence reports**: who the [companion](/companion/install) saw in an instance while a moderator
with the client was there. VRChat does not report *who* is in an instance any other way.

**Modbot only knows who was in an instance while a moderator's companion was reporting from it.**
Nobody with the client in the instance means no presence reports at all, and every figure built on
them then reads as nought however busy the instance really was. That is not a fault and it is not
an estimate: it is the limit of what VRChat will tell anybody.

What this means for a figure drawn over a range:

- A world nobody with the client visited looks empty.
- "The most people seen in one instance" is the most **seen**, which is at most the most there were.
- A busy Saturday nobody covered leaves no trace, so it cannot pull an average or a peak up.

Where a page's figures rest on presence reports, it counts them for you and says so when they are
thin: **No presence reports in this range**, or **Only 84 presence reports in this range**. Treat
anything below a few hundred as a sample rather than a measurement.

### Head counts

Some figures (**People in instances**, and the peaks on the [Instances](#instances) page) come from
**head counts** instead, and these are not limited by who was in the instance. Modbot reads each of
the group's open instances' own page about every thirty seconds and keeps VRChat's own count of how
many people are in it.

No moderator and no companion is involved, so head counts cover instances nobody from the team was
standing in. What they cannot tell you is *who* those people were — only how many. That is why the
two sources sit side by side rather than one replacing the other.

A count is stored only when the number **changes**, so an instance that sits at twelve people all
evening is one stored reading, not a thousand. Charts drawn from head counts are therefore a
staircase: each reading holds until the next one.

Head counts start when Modbot starts reading an instance, so they can still be incomplete:

- An instance that opened before Modbot was set up, or while the group's instance list could not be
  read, has no counts for that part of its life.
- If VRChat rate limits Modbot, reading stops until the limit clears and nothing is counted in
  between. [Health](/self-hosting/health) shows when that is happening.

The Instances page measures exactly this and says so — see [Coverage](#coverage) below.

## VRChat

**Is the community growing or shrinking, and what changed?**

The stats along the top:

- **Members**: the group's member count as VRChat last reported it, with the date it was read.
- **Joined** and **Left**: joins and leaves recorded in the range.
- **Net change**: joined minus left in the range.

Then the two peaks, each with the moment it happened, in your own time zone:

- **Most members**: the highest member count VRChat reported in the range.
- **Most online at once**: the highest number of the group's members who were in VRChat at the same
  moment, anywhere — not only in your instances.

Both are read from the five-minute member count readings, so the moment shown is a real reading and
not a day. Where the same number was reached more than once, the **earliest** time is shown, so the
figure does not move about between two loads of the same page.

**What the peaks rest on.** Modbot can only find a peak among the readings it has. If the range
reaches back further than Modbot has been running, or [retention](/self-hosting/data-retention) has
deleted the older readings, the peak is the highest Modbot saw and not necessarily the highest there
was. When fewer than half the days in the range carry a reading, the page says so above the stats —
**Readings on 2 of 30 days in this range** — and with no readings at all, **No member count readings
in this range**.

Below them, the [**Insights**](/ai/insights) panel appears if AI insights are set up.

| Panel | What it shows | Comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Member count** | The member count VRChat reported, reading by reading, with the number of members online in VRChat drawn over it in green on its own scale at the right. Hover a point for both numbers and the time it was read. | Member count readings |
| **Joins and leaves per day** | Joins and leaves, side by side, per day. | Daily totals |
| **Joined minus left, running** | A running total of recorded joins minus recorded leaves, counted from zero on the first day Modbot recorded. This is not the member count: a group that already had 5,000 members when Modbot started still starts at zero. | Daily totals |
| **Invites and join requests** | **Invites sent**; **Invites accepted** (the share of invited people who joined within 7 days); **Join requests**; **Requests decided** (approved / rejected); and a chart of invites and requests per day. | Daily totals, except invites accepted (fact log) |
| **Roles** | Each role VRChat lists for the group, with how many times it was **Given** and **Taken away** in the range. Badges mark **moderation** roles, roles **given on join** and **self-service** roles. Shows when the role list was last read. | Fact log |
| **How long members have been members** | Current members grouped into **Under a week**, **1 to 4 weeks**, **1 to 3 months**, **3 to 12 months** and **Over a year**. | Fact log |

**Member count** has range buttons of its own: **Day**, **Week** (the default), **Month** and
**All**. Modbot reads the group's member count and online member count about every five minutes
and keeps every reading. Long ranges show up to about 500 of them, spread evenly across the range;
before the first stored reading, the chart shows one point per day from the group's recorded
changes. Readings older than the presence [retention](/self-hosting/data-retention) window are
deleted if one is set.

**How long members have been members** ignores the range: it is about who is a member now. It only
counts members whose join Modbot recorded, and says how many that is. Members who joined before
Modbot started recording are not counted here.

## Discord

**Is the Discord server healthy, and who keeps it going?**

The Discord page needs the Discord bot. The stats along the top are **Members**, **Messages** in the
range, **Active in 30 days** (with the share of members) and **Time in voice** in the range. The page
then shows the member count, joins and leaves, messages and voice minutes per day, active members
(per day, 7 days or 30 days), the busiest channels and hours, moderation actions per day, new
members who stayed, member health and top contributors. Names open that person's popup.

"Active" means the person sent a message or spent time in voice. Bots are never counted.

Most of it comes from daily totals. **New members still here** pairs joins with later leaves, so it
covers what the fact log still holds. **Member health** is about now and ignores the range.

For every panel in detail, and how messages are stored, see
[Discord messages and analytics](/discord/messages-and-analytics).

## Team

**Who is doing the moderation work, and when is nobody covering?**

The stats along the top:

- **Moderators active**: how many people took a moderation action in the range.
- **Actions**: all moderation actions in the range.
- **Coverage gaps**: how many gaps were found (see below).
- **Instances nobody watched**: group instances opened with no companion ever in them, out of
  all instances opened.

### Coverage gaps

A coverage gap is a stretch of time when people were in a group instance and no moderator was. It
starts when the last moderator leaves while people are still there, and ends when a moderator is seen
again or the instance closes.

A moderator counts as present when their companion is reporting from the instance, or when
another client sees someone Modbot recognises as a moderator (they hold a role with moderation
permissions, have taken a moderation action, or own the group). A moderator without the client, in
an instance no client is in, cannot be seen at all.

Use the buttons to show only gaps where at least a number of people were left behind:
**Anyone left behind**, **3+ people** (the default), **5+** or **10+**. The table shows:

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Began** | When the last moderator left. |
| **Lasted** | How long until the gap ended, or "unknown". |
| **People left behind** | How many people were there when the last moderator left. |
| **Last moderator out** | Who left last, or "a client stopped reporting". |
| **Ended because** | "a moderator arrived", "the instance was closed", or "nothing more was reported". |
| **Instance** | The world (click to open it) and instance number. |

### Actions

- **Actions per moderator**: one row per moderator with a **Total** and a column per kind of action:
  **Instance kicks**, **Warns**, **Bans**, **Unbans**, **Removed from group**, **Invites**, **Requests
  approved**, **Requests rejected** and **Role changes**, plus the day they were **Last active**.
  Click a name to open their [popup](/moderation/popups). People who can review tickets also see a
  **Reviews of unusual patterns →** button that opens [Reviews](/moderation/reviews).
- **Actions per day**: all actions per day.
- **What kind of actions**: each kind of action, largest first.

The action counts come from daily totals. Coverage gaps come from presence reports in the fact log.

## Worlds

**Which of our worlds actually get used?**

If there are no presence reports in the range, the page says **No presence reports in this range.**
If there are fewer than 200, it says how many, as a reminder that the figures are thin.

The stats along the top are **Worlds**, **Time seen**, **Visitors** and **Presence reports**.

**Worlds, by time people were seen in them** lists each world with:

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **World** | Name, author and world id. Click the name to open the world. |
| **Holds** | The world's capacity. |
| **Time seen** | Total time people were seen in the world. |
| **Visitors** | Different people seen there. |
| **Arrivals seen** | Times someone was seen arriving. |
| **Instances opened** | Group instances opened in the world. |
| **Last seen** | When someone was last seen there. |

**Visitors per day, busiest worlds** charts visitors per day for the five busiest worlds.

Time, visitors and arrivals come from presence reports. **Instances opened** comes from the group's
audit log and is complete whether or not a client was there. Visitors per day comes from daily totals
and outlasts a short retention window.

## Instances

**When is the community actually active?**

The stats along the top are **Opened** and **Closed** (instances in the range), **Typical time open**
and **Most open at once**.

### Peaks

Under them is a second row of peaks, each with the moment it happened, in your own time zone. All
four are built from [head counts](#head-counts) — VRChat's own numbers — so they cover instances
nobody from the team was standing in.

| Stat | What it counts |
|---|---|
| **Most people at once** | The most people in all of the group's instances added together, at one moment. |
| **Fullest instance** | The most people in a single instance at one moment, with the world it was in. |
| **Busiest day** | The day with the most **people-time**, shown as hours. |
| **Busiest hour** | The single clock hour with the most people-time, on a real date — not "Saturdays at 8". |

**People-time** is people added up over the minutes they were there: one person for an hour and
sixty people for one minute are both one hour. Busiest is measured that way rather than by the peak,
because a night that touched forty people for five minutes is not a busier night than one that held
twenty for six hours. For "Saturdays at 8" — the same hour across weeks — use **When the community
is active** below.

Where the same height was reached twice, the **earliest** moment is shown, so the figure does not
move about between two loads of the same page. A range with nothing recorded shows a dash rather
than a zero: nothing was recorded is not the same statement as the peak was nought.

### Coverage

A peak is only as good as the time Modbot was counting. Two hours of counting in a week gives a
peak that is real and is **not** the week's peak, and nothing about the number itself says which one
you are looking at. So the page measures it.

Modbot compares two amounts of time inside the range:

- how long the group's instances were **open**, added up across instances, and
- how much of that time it actually **had a head count** for — an instance counts from its first
  stored reading onwards, and one Modbot never read contributes nothing.

When the second is less than half the first, the page says so above the stats: **Head counts cover
19% of the time instances were open**. With no head counts at all in the range, it says **No head
counts in this range**.

The peaks are still shown when coverage is thin. They are marked, not hidden — a real peak from a
short window is still worth seeing, as long as you know that is what it is.

### Panels

| Panel | What it shows | Comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Open right now** | Group instances open at this moment. Shown only when there are some, and ignores the range. | Modbot's instance list |
| **Recent instances** | The most recent instances. | Modbot's instance list |
| **People in instances** | People in all of the group's instances, moment by moment, with how many instances they were spread across drawn over it in green on its own scale at the right. Hover a point for both numbers and the time. | Head counts |
| **When the community is active** | A week-by-hour grid. Switch between **People arriving** and **Instances opened**. Hours are shown in your own time zone (named in the title), rounded to whole hours. | Fact log (arrivals from presence reports) |
| **Opened and closed per day** | Instances opened and closed each day. | Daily totals |
| **Most open at once, per day** | The most group instances open at the same time each day. | Fact log |
| **Most people at once, per day** | The most people in all of the group's instances at one moment, each day. | Head counts |
| **Busy time per day** | People-time per day, in hours. A long steady evening reads higher than a short rush. | Head counts |
| **Typical time open, per day** | The middle time from open to close, over the instances that closed that day. The stat at the top is the same figure for the whole range. | Fact log |
| **Most people seen in one instance, per day** | The biggest crowd a moderator's companion **saw** in a single instance each day. | Presence reports |

**People in instances** has range buttons of its own: **Day**, **Week** (the default), **Month** and
**All**, because it is drawn from readings taken every thirty seconds rather than from whole days.
Long ranges show up to about 500 readings spread evenly across the range, and every point is a
number VRChat reported at the time shown. The line is a staircase because a count is stored only
when it changes: each reading holds until the next.

**Most people at once, per day** and **Most people seen in one instance, per day** look similar and
are not the same figure. The first is every instance added together, from VRChat's own counts, and
is complete whether or not anyone was watching. The second is one instance at a time, from what a
companion actually saw, and is nought on a day nobody with the client was around. If the range has
fewer than 200 presence reports the page says so, and with none it says so in that panel too.

VRChat only records an instance closing when a moderator closes it. An instance that emptied out on
its own has no close on record, so Modbot treats it as open until the last thing seen in it (a kick,
a warning or a presence report). That is why **Closed** is often lower than **Opened**, and why
**Typical time open** only covers instances with both ends on record.
