title: Alerts by email
description: Getting an email when Modbot stops working - what it can tell you itself, what Modbot Cloud tells you when it cannot, and how to set both up.

Modbot can email you when it stops working. There are two halves, and you want both, because they
see different things.

| Half | Watches | Sends through | Sees |
|---|---|---|---|
| **In Modbot** | VRChat, the Discord bot, sync, storage, AI spend, email, logs to Cloud | your own SMTP | everything a running Modbot knows about itself |
| **In Modbot Cloud** | whether your Modbot has gone quiet, and errors in its log | Modbot Cloud's own mail | the one thing Modbot cannot report: that it is not running |

<Callout type="info" title="Why two">
A Modbot that is down cannot email you about being down. The process that would send the mail is
the process that is not running, and the database holding your address is the one that cannot be
reached. That is the whole reason the Cloud half exists.
</Callout>

## In Modbot

**Settings**, **Integrations**, **Health alerts**. You need the **Change settings** permission, and
[email set up](/self-hosting/environment-variables) — the same SMTP the reset links use.

Everything is off until you turn it on.

### What it can watch

| Check | Fires when |
|---|---|
| **VRChat** | Modbot cannot reach VRChat and waiting will not fix it, or it has been waiting more than an hour |
| **Discord bot** | the bot is disconnected or stopped |
| **Sync** | the group's audit log has not been read for three hours, and a group is set up |
| **Storage** | the database has grown past the size you asked to be told about |
| **AI spend** | a spending limit has been reached |
| **Email** | an email could not be sent, or one has been waiting more than three hours |
| **Logs to Modbot Cloud** | sending has been failing for more than three hours |

**Database bigger than (GB)** is the storage check's own number. `0` turns that check off. It is not
the disk size on the **Host & Database** screen — that one is a what-if input your browser
remembers, this one is a line you want to hear about.

### Who is emailed

The staff accounts you tick. An account with no email address cannot be ticked, and a disabled
account is skipped.

Mail goes out through the same queue as everything else Modbot sends, so it counts against the
[daily email limit](/accounts/staff-accounts) and waits its turn if the limit is reached.

### How often it repeats itself

Each check has a state, so:

- going wrong sends **one** email;
- staying wrong sends nothing until the **quiet time** is up — 6 hours by default — and then says it
  again, because a problem nobody has fixed is still a problem;
- coming back sends **one** email saying it is over.

The checks run every five minutes, starting two minutes after Modbot does — a Modbot that has just
started has not finished a sync pass yet.

Turning a check off clears what it was saying, so turning it back on later does not open with a
recovery email about a problem you never heard about.

## In Modbot Cloud

Set up in Modbot Cloud, under **Logs**, by you once you have claimed your server, or by a Modbot
Cloud administrator. Leave the address empty and it goes to the address on your Cloud account, so
there is nothing to keep up to date when you change it.

It needs your Modbot to be [sending its logs to Cloud](/self-hosting/logs#sending-logs-to-modbot-cloud),
which is on by default. That is what Cloud watches: if the logs stop arriving, something is wrong
with the deployment, and Cloud is the only side that can see it.

| Check | Fires when |
|---|---|
| **Quiet** | nothing has arrived from your Modbot for longer than the window — an hour by default |
| **Errors** | your Modbot wrote more errors in the last hour than the number set. `0` turns it off |

The same state rules apply: said once, repeated after the quiet time, and said to be over.

**Silence is not proof of a problem.** Turning sending off, or setting `MODBOT_CLOUD_DISABLED`, also
makes a deployment go quiet. That is why this check is off unless somebody turns it on, and why the
email says what Cloud saw rather than what it concluded.

An email Cloud could not send does not start the quiet time, so an outage at the mail provider does
not swallow the one message that mattered.
