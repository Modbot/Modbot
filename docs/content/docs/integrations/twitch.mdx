Modbot can watch your group's Twitch channel. Once a minute it asks Twitch whether the channel is
live. While it is, the **Live** and **Now** pages show a **Live on Twitch** card, each stream is
linked to the [calendar event](/moderation/calendar) that was on at the time, and Modbot can post
**We're live on Twitch** to Discord, your VRChat group and Bluesky.

It is free and **nobody signs in to Twitch**: you make a small Twitch app of your own and give its
client id and secret to Modbot. Modbot uses them only to read the channel's public live status.
Your Modbot does not need a public address for this.

Everything here is in **Settings**, **Twitch**, and needs
[**Change settings**](/accounts/roles-and-permissions#change-settings).

## Make the Twitch app

You make it once, on Twitch's developer site, signed in as any Twitch account. It does not have to
be the channel's account.

1. Turn on two-factor authentication for that Twitch account. Twitch will not let you register an
   app without it.
2. Go to the [Twitch developer console](https://dev.twitch.tv/console) and click **Register Your
   Application**.
3. Give it a **Name**. It must be different from every other Twitch app's, so use something of your
   group's, such as `OurGroup Modbot`.
4. For **OAuth Redirect URLs** enter `http://localhost`. Twitch asks for one, but Modbot never signs
   anyone in, so it is never used.
5. Pick a **Category**, tick the box that says you are not a robot, and click **Create**.
6. Click **Manage** on your new app. Copy the **Client ID**.
7. Click **New Secret** and copy it. Twitch shows the secret once. Treat it like a password.

## Set it up in Modbot

In Modbot, go to **Settings**, **Twitch**:

1. Paste the **Client id** and the **Client secret**.
2. Type the **Channel**: its name, such as `ourgroup`, or its address, such as
   `https://www.twitch.tv/ourgroup`.
3. Click **Save**. The page shows **Client secret: Saved**, and never shows it again.
4. Click **Check**.
5. When Check passes, the **Live** switch appears. Turn it on.

### What Check shows

Check asks Twitch for an app token with the client id and secret, finds the channel by its name, and
asks whether the token may read the channel's stream. **It never changes anything on Twitch.**

| Line | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Channel** | The channel's name as Twitch shows it. |

When something is wrong, Check says what:

| What it says | What to do |
|---|---|
| Twitch did not accept the client id and secret. | Copy them again from the developer console. If you made a new secret, the old one stops working. |
| Twitch has no channel with that name. | Check the spelling of the channel. |
| Twitch did not accept the sign-in. | Press **Check** again. |
| Twitch did not answer. | Twitch, or the connection to it, failed. Try again later. |
| Twitch is limiting Modbot. Try again after … | Twitch asked Modbot to slow down. Nothing is sent to Twitch until then, **Check** included. |

What Check found stays on the page until you change the client id, the secret or the channel. Change
any of them and Check again; the live checks wait until it passes.

## Live on Twitch

With **Live** on, the **Live** and **Now** pages show a **Live on Twitch** card while the channel is
streaming, for people who have
[**See live instances**](/accounts/roles-and-permissions#see-live-instances). It shows:

- the stream's title and category,
- how long it has been live,
- the viewer count (Twitch's own public number),
- the event it is linked to, if any,
- **Open**, which opens the channel on Twitch.

The card is not there while the channel is not live. It changes by itself when the title or category
changes, and the viewer count moves on about every five minutes.

Modbot sees a stream start within about a minute. Twitch can take a little longer to say so.

**Live** is also what the **Integrations** page and **Health** read: **Integrations** shows Twitch as
**Needs setup**, **Set up** (Check passed, **Live** off) or **Live**. **Health** has a **Twitch** card
when Check or the live checks found a problem, when Twitch is limiting Modbot, or when it has not
answered for over five minutes.

## Events

When a stream starts, Modbot links it to the one [calendar event](/moderation/calendar) that was on:
from an hour before the event starts to its end. A repeating event is checked by its dates, and a
date cancelled on its own does not count. **When two events overlap, Modbot does not guess: the
stream is left unlinked.** An event made after the stream began is picked up while the stream is
still live.

The event shows **Streamed on Twitch**, with the time and **Open** for the channel. Somebody with
**Manage calendar** can click **Remove** to take a stream off the event, or **Link a Twitch stream**
to pick another from the channel's recent streams. What a person chose, Modbot never changes.

## The live post

Under **Live post** you decide what **We're live on Twitch** says and where it goes.

**Nothing is ticked to start.** Until you tick **Discord**, **VRChat** or **Bluesky**, no post is made.
Each site has the same choices as on the [**Marketing**](/moderation/marketing) page: a Discord
channel, role and **Publish to followers**; who sees a VRChat post, its roles and **Notify members**.

### The words

Write a **Title** and some **Text**. The buttons **Stream title**, **Category** and **Link** put a
word in that Modbot swaps for the real one when it makes the post:

| Word | Becomes |
|---|---|
| `{title}` | The stream's title |
| `{category}` | The stream's category |
| `{link}` | The channel's address on Twitch |

Clear a field and **Save** to go back to the built-in words: the title **We're live on Twitch**, and
the stream's title and link as the text. VRChat needs a title; Bluesky shows the title and text as
one post of up to 300 characters.

### When it posts

- Only a stream Twitch calls **live** posts. Reruns, premieres, playlists and watch parties never do.
- One post for each Twitch stream, however many times Modbot looks. A stream that drops offline and
  comes back under the same Twitch id is still one stream.
- A stream Modbot first sees more than 15 minutes after it started does not post: "we're live" would
  be old news.

| Setting | Built-in | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Minutes live** | 3 | The stream must have been live this long first, so a test stream or a dropout does not post. 1 to 60. |
| **Hours between posts** | 6 | No second post inside this many hours of the last one, whatever the stream. 1 to 168. |

The post is made when the stream has been live long enough, and then it is an ordinary post: it
shows on the **Marketing** page, the same sending puts it on each site, at most once, and **Try
again** and **Pause all posting** work as they do for any post. Posts hold while **Pause all
posting** is on, and **a "live" post more than 15 minutes late is not sent**: it shows **Failed**
with **Not sent on time.**, because a live post an hour late is wrong. It is not edited or removed
when the stream ends: it stays as the record.

## Forget

**Forget** asks first, then removes the client id, the secret, the channel and what Check found, and
turns **Live** off. The live post's words, times and ticked sites stay. Streams Modbot already
recorded stay too. To stop the app working at Twitch's end as well, delete it in the developer
console or make a new secret.

## What leaves your server

| Sent | To | When |
|---|---|---|
| The client id and secret | `id.twitch.tv`, to get an app token | **Check**, and about once every two months |
| The app token, the client id and the channel's name or id | `api.twitch.tv` | **Check**, and once a minute while **Live** is on |
| A post's title and text, with the stream's title, category and the channel's link filled in | The sites you ticked, and from there to their readers | Only when the live post is made |

Twitch sees that your server asks about the channel, which is public. **Nothing about your members**
goes to Twitch or into the post. Nothing is sent to Modbot Cloud. See
[Privacy](/privacy#what-leaves-your-server) and [Security](/security#secrets-stored-in-the-database).

When Twitch rate limits Modbot, Modbot stops every request to Twitch until the time Twitch says, and
never sends the request again before then.
