---
title: The SteamVR overlay
description: The headset panel the companion draws for SteamVR, what it shows, what it needs, and its limits today.
---

The companion includes a SteamVR overlay: a panel meant to show a moderator, inside the
headset, who is in the group instance they are standing in and whether someone with a history of
kicks or bans has just walked in. Inside VRChat you cannot see email, Discord or a browser, so this
panel is how Modbot reaches you there.

The overlay is part of the same client you [install](/companion/install) and
[pair](/companion/pairing). There is nothing extra to download and nothing to switch on. If you do
not want it, [turn it off](#turning-it-off).

## What it needs

- **SteamVR**, or on Linux **WiVRn or Monado** (see [below](#wivrn-and-monado)). The companion
  never starts any of them itself. It looks for a running one every ten seconds and attaches the
  panel when it finds one; when the runtime closes, the panel lets go and comes back the next time
  it is started. Start them in either order.
- **A paired server** for the group whose instance you are in.
- **You in one of that group's instances** for the group's roster, flags and alerts. In a public,
  friends-only or private instance the panel still lists the people from your own copy of VRChat's
  log (see [Outside group instances](#outside-group-instances)). With no server paired, or in a
  group instance none of your servers manages, the panel is blank (see [Idle screen](#idle-screen))
  and no server is contacted.

Without SteamVR, or without a headset, the client simply runs without an overlay and keeps
reporting presence as normal. The overlay never stops the rest of the client from working: if it
cannot start, the client carries on without it. A panel that fails to start (for example because
the graphics card is out of memory) shows **Could not start** on the **SteamVR** page with a short
reason, and the client tries it again once a minute, up to five times in all. Switching the panel
off and on again, or restarting Modbot, starts the count again.

On a PC with more than one graphics card, the panel is drawn on the card SteamVR says the headset
is on, and only that card: if it cannot make room for the panel, the panel shows **Could not
start** rather than being drawn on a card the headset cannot show. Without SteamVR (the debug
mode preview), it uses the real graphics card with the most memory of its own, skipping
screen-sharing programs' and Windows' stand-in adapters.

The client window's **SteamVR** page shows where the overlay stands: attached (and to what),
not running, not installed, refused (with the runtime's reason) or could not start (with the
reason), when it attached, how many
frames it has drawn, what it is showing right now, and the panel's size and position. To see the
panel's picture on your desktop without a headset, start the client in
[debug mode](/companion/settings#debug-mode).

### WiVRn and Monado

On Linux with WiVRn or Monado, the companion draws the same panel through OpenXR instead of
SteamVR. It needs the OpenXR loader (`libopenxr_loader.so.1`) and Vulkan, both of which a machine
running WiVRn or Monado already has; there is nothing to install. xrizer alone is not enough,
because it runs OpenVR games only and turns overlay programs away, so the companion goes to the
OpenXR runtime itself. When the panel is up, the **SteamVR** page reads **Attached to WiVRn.** (or
the name of the runtime it found).

## Turning it off

The **SteamVR** page has an **Overlay on** switch at the top. Turn it off and the main panel
stops: nothing is drawn, your controllers are not read, and SteamVR is let go. The sidebar reads
**off** beside it.

The placement settings stay on the page, so you can arrange where the panel will sit before you
turn it on. What goes away is only what has nothing to report while it is off: how long it has
been attached, how many frames it has drawn, and what it is showing.

There are four panels and each has its own switch: this one, the
[notification overlay](/companion/settings#notification-overlay), the
[desktop overlay](/companion/settings#desktop-overlay), and the
[notification overlay on a monitor](/companion/settings#notification-overlay-on-a-monitor). None of
them can take another down. The live connection to your group's server is held open while any of
the four is on and closes when the last one goes off.

The rest of the client is unaffected either way: it goes on reading VRChat's log and reporting
presence to every server you paired with.

Turn it back on and the panel comes up again where you left it, without restarting the client.
The setting is remembered in `settings.json` as `overlayOn`, in your own user folder, so it
survives both restarts and updates.

## The Modbot tab in the SteamVR dashboard

Press the system button to open the SteamVR dashboard, and Modbot has a button of its own there
that opens its tab, the way OVR Advanced Settings does. It holds the overlay settings, so you can change them
without taking the headset off:

| Card | What is on it |
|---|---|
| **Overlay** | **Overlay on**, the main panel's switch, with [**Put it back in front of me**](#put-it-back-in-front-of-me) beside it, and **Fixed to**: Head, Left wrist, Right wrist or Room |
| **Notifications** | **Notification overlay on**, **Where on the screen** (the six spots), and the **Pop-up** ticks, one for each kind of event |
| **Placement** | The notification overlay's **Across**, **Down**, **Distance**, **Width**, **Opacity** and **Pop-up stays** sliders, each with a minus and a plus |

Point at it with SteamVR's own laser and pull the trigger. A slider follows the laser while the
trigger is held. These are the same settings as the window's **SteamVR** page and the
[Tell me about](/companion/settings#tell-me-about) card's **Pop-up** column, saved to the same
fields of `settings.json`, so a change in either place shows in the other.

The tab is there whenever SteamVR is running, with either panel on or off, so it is also where you
switch the main panel back on from inside the headset. Like the panels, the companion never starts
SteamVR to put it there. WiVRn and Monado have no dashboard, so there is no tab on them.

## What it shows

### Whose group it is

Across the top, above the tabs: your group's icon and your group's name, as the server gave them
when you paired. If a pairing was made before servers said their group's name, the server's address
is shown instead.

### The roster

A list of who is in the instance right now, as your group's server knows it. At the top it says how
many people are here and how fresh the list is.

People are sorted so the rows that matter come first:

| Dot | Who |
|---|---|
| Red | Flagged: one of your group's [flag rules](/moderation/live#who-is-flagged) matches them. The row shows what matched, such as `2 kicks or bans` or `Nuisance`. |
| Green | A member of the group. |
| Grey | Everyone else. |

The drawing code also has a place for staff, sorted after flagged people, but the server does not
mark anyone as staff yet.

If there is nobody to list, it says **Nobody here.** If no list has been loaded for this instance
yet, it says **No roster loaded for this instance.**

When somebody leaves, their row stays on the list for 60 seconds, greyed and tagged **Left**, with
the seconds still to go where their join time was. Tapping it still opens their card. They are not
counted in how many are here, and if they come back the row goes back to normal.

### The Audit Log tab

Despite its name this is not your group's audit log (that is on the web app). It is the live feed
for the instance you are in, newest first: who joined, who left, who was already here, flagged joins
and a watch ending. It is made from the same live connection as the roster.

Somebody who was already in the instance when you arrived reads `(here before you) ~5m+` on the
Instance list, the Audit Log and the wrist panel: they have been there at least as long as you have,
so the time is how long you have been in the instance, in whole minutes (`~1hr 4m+` from an hour on).
If your log has no record of when you arrived, for example because the overlay was switched on after
you got there, it says `(here before you)` alone rather than guessing.

### Flagged user card

When someone one of your group's [flag rules](/moderation/live#who-is-flagged) matches joins the
instance you are in, a card appears above the roster: **Flagged user joined**, followed by your
group's name, then the person's display name and a line saying what matched, such as
`1 kick or ban · 5 warns`.

Every paired moderator in that instance gets the card, including the one whose client reported the
join. VRChat's log only says that someone joined; whether your group has kicked or banned them
before is something only the server knows, so your own client is warned too.

### Problem banner

A yellow banner at the top appears only when reporting has really stopped:

- *… rejected this device. Reporting and lookups have stopped; if you were removed from that
  group's staff, this is expected.* See [When a token is revoked](/companion/pairing#when-a-token-is-revoked).
- *Cannot reach …. Showing what was last known.* The server could not be reached and the roster is
  no longer fresh.

### Save a clip

With [Clips](/companion/clips) switched on, a wide control sits under the tabs: **Save a clip**.
Pressing it keeps the last few minutes of VRChat, picture and sound, as a video file on your own PC.
That page says what the sound is. When nothing is
being kept it says why instead — *Waiting for VRChat*, *The folder cannot be used* — and cannot be
pressed; after a clip is written it says **Clip saved** for a few seconds. With Clips switched off
there is no control at all.

### Outside group instances

In a public, friends-only or private instance the group's server knows nothing about the room, so
the panel works from your own copy of VRChat's log instead. It says **Not in a group instance**
across the top and **Not synced with the group** under it, and there are no rank, flags or heads-up
**+** on it, because those come from the group:

- **Instance** lists the people the log says are here, with how long each has been here, and keeps
  the **Left** rows for a minute as above.
- **Audit Log** lists who joined, who left and who was already here, as your log reports it. There
  are no flagged joins, because those come from the group's server.
- Tapping a person still opens their card from the server.

No roster is read and no live connection is opened for these instances, and nothing about the
instance (its id or its world) is sent. The one request that goes out is the person's card, which
names the person and nothing else. With several servers paired it goes to the server whose group
you were last in an instance of since the client started, or the first paired one if you have not
been in any.

### Idle screen

When there is nothing to show, the panel shows nothing at all, and the client contacts no server
for it. That is when you are not in any instance yet, when VRChat's log has stopped, when you are in
a group instance none of your paired servers manages, and when no server is paired. The **idle**
sample on the [debug page](/companion/settings#debug-mode) draws a card reading **Not in a group
instance** in the panel's place, so you can see where it sits. The panel draws the same card by
itself for 5 seconds after you press
[**Put it back in front of me**](#put-it-back-in-front-of-me), and then goes blank again.

**Save a clip** is the one thing still shown there, when Clips is on, because the companion records
wherever VRChat is running and a moment worth keeping can happen in a public instance too.

## Holding it

The panel can be moved with a controller:

| Do this | And |
|---|---|
| Point at the panel | A ring shows where you are pointing. |
| Squeeze the grip while pointing | The panel comes with your hand. An ordinary hold is enough; you do not have to squeeze hard. Let go and it stays where you left it in the room, or on your wrist if you brought it to your hand. |
| Thumbstick up or down while holding | Pushes the panel away or pulls it closer. |
| Thumbstick left or right while holding | Makes it narrower or wider. Either hand's thumbstick works while one hand holds it. |
| Squeeze the other grip on the panel while holding | Pull your hands apart to make it bigger, bring them together to make it smaller. |
| Grip twice quickly on the panel | Puts it back in front of your head, at a readable size. |
| Any of the above, with the hand the panel is [worn on](#on-your-wrist) | Nothing. That hand ignores the panel it is carrying; use the other one. |
| Pull the trigger on the panel | Taps what you are pointing at: the alert card to dismiss it, a person's row to open their card, the card to close it, **Save a clip** to keep the last few minutes. |
| Thumbstick up or down over the list | Scrolls the roster. |

Where you leave the panel, its size, opacity and curve are saved and used next time. The
**SteamVR** page has the same settings, and **Put it back in front of me**.

### Put it back in front of me

For when you cannot find the panel. It is on the window's **SteamVR** page, in the command
palette, and on the **Overlay** card of the [Modbot tab](#the-modbot-tab-in-the-steamvr-dashboard)
in the SteamVR dashboard, so you can reach it from inside the headset. Pressing it:

- switches the main panel on if it was off, and shows it again if you had said "hide overlay";
- fixes it to your head, straight ahead at 0.8 m and a little below your eyes;
- sets the width back to 0.45 m, opacity to 100% and curve to 0%, and turns the lock and
  click-through off;
- shows the **Not in a group instance** card for 5 seconds if the panel would otherwise be blank.

Width, opacity, curve, the lock and click-through are all reset, not kept as they were.

### The bar under the panel

While you point at the panel a small bar comes up under it with two buttons:

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| Lock | The panel can no longer be picked up, moved or resized. Tapping and scrolling still work. Press it again to unlock. The padlock is shut and purple while locked. |
| Hand | Click-through: the panel ignores your pointer, so pointing and clicking go to VRChat behind it. The bar still answers, so you can press the hand again to turn it off. The hand is struck through and purple while it is on. |

Both are saved with the panel and kept when you restart. The notification panel has the same bar,
and so does the [desktop overlay](/companion/settings#desktop-overlay), in the strip along its top.

## On your wrist

The **Placement** card's **Fixed to** row offers **Head**, **Left wrist**, **Right wrist** and
**Room**. Choosing a wrist puts the panel on that arm, facing out of the back of your hand, at
0.16 metres across — something to glance at like a watch rather than a window to work in. You can
also get there with a controller: carry the panel to your hand and let go of the grip, and it
settles onto that wrist.

On a wrist the panel shows four lines instead of the roster, because at that size a list of names
is unreadable:

- your group's icon and name,
- how many people are here, in yellow when the list is no longer fresh,
- a Modbot fault if there is one; otherwise a flagged arrival, name first with the reason under
  it; otherwise the newest join or leave,
- nothing else.

**The hand wearing the panel ignores it.** It does not point at it, tap it, scroll it or grab it,
so the panel is not in the way of that hand and squeezing that grip — for any of the reasons you
squeeze a grip in VRChat — leaves the panel alone.

Use your **other hand**: point at it as you would at any panel, and a tap anywhere on a flagged
arrival clears it. To get the roster back, grip the panel twice quickly with that other hand, or
choose **Head** or **Room** on the **SteamVR** page. If the controller wearing the panel is
switched off, the panel has nothing to hang from, so move it from that page.

On WiVRn and Monado the same works through OpenXR. The Valve Index, Oculus and Meta Touch, and
HTC Vive controllers are bound: grip holds, trigger taps, and the thumbstick or trackpad scrolls
and resizes. Any other controller gets OpenXR's simple controller bindings, where the select
button taps and the menu button holds, and there is no scrolling.

## How it follows you

The overlay uses the same reading of VRChat's log as the rest of the client, so it knows which
instance you are in without asking anyone. If you are paired with several groups, it shows only the
server whose group owns the instance you are standing in. Walking into another instance clears any
card about the one you left.

If VRChat's log stops growing for about two minutes, the client treats you as no longer in that
instance and the panel goes back to idle.

## How fresh it is

The panel always says how old its list is:

| It says | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **up to date** | Loaded in the last 45 seconds. |
| **as of N seconds ago** / **as of N minutes ago** / **as of N hours ago** | Older than that. Shown in yellow. |
| **not loaded** | Nothing has been loaded. |

The panel is drawn from what the client already holds, never from a request made while drawing. If
the server is slow or unreachable, you see the last list with its age, rather than a blank panel.
That list is kept in memory only, never written to disk, and is thrown away when you unpair the
server or quit the client.

## Limits

- **One card at a time.** A flagged user card stays up for 25 seconds, then clears itself.
- **The same person raises at most one card every five minutes**, so someone rejoining again and
  again does not flood the panel.
- **Only cards for your current instance.** A flagged join in a different instance is dropped, not
  saved for later.
- **The roster follows the instance.** While you are in a group instance the companion keeps a
  [live connection](/api/live-updates) to that group's server; a join or a leave re-reads the
  roster at once, and it is re-read every 20 seconds regardless.
- **Nothing on the panel moderates.** The trigger dismisses a card, opens a person's card, closes
  it again, and — if you have [Clips](/companion/clips) switched on — saves a clip to your own PC.
  That is all: there are no ban, kick or warn buttons, and the client's token could not do those
  things anyway. To act on something, use the web app.
- **The panel starts 0.45 metres wide** in the headset, 0.16 metres on a wrist, and is drawn at
  1024 by 1024 pixels either way.
- **Long names are cut to one line**, so a display name full of line breaks cannot push the rest of
  the panel out of view.
- **SteamVR, WiVRn or Monado, on Windows or Linux.** The panel is an OpenVR overlay for SteamVR
  and an OpenXR overlay session for the other two; VRChat and the runtime run on the same machine
  as the companion. Other OpenXR runtimes, SteamVR's own included, have no overlay extension, so
  the companion has nothing to hand them.
- **It starts below and to the right of your view**, about an arm's length away, following your
  head. Move it with a controller or from the **SteamVR** page; see
  [Holding it](#holding-it) and [On your wrist](#on-your-wrist).

## The desktop overlay and its bubble

The [desktop overlay](/companion/settings#desktop-overlay) is the same panel in a window on a
monitor. While VRChat's window is there, the window sits in the free space to the right of
VRChat's own Esc menu and gets bigger or smaller with VRChat's window, so it does not cover the
menu. Modbot also puts a small bubble in VRChat's top-left row of key hints, after the Y one,
showing the key that opens the overlay (`Ctrl+Alt+M` unless you changed it). It is purple while
the overlay is open, and clicking it opens and closes the overlay, the same as the key. The bubble
is there whenever the desktop overlay is on and VRChat's window is showing, and is gone when
either is not.

To do this the client asks Windows where VRChat's window is, how big it is and whether it is
minimised. It reads no pixels, no keys and nothing from inside VRChat's window, it does not
move, resize or press anything in it, and nothing about it is sent anywhere.

## What it sends

Nothing at all while the overlay is [switched off](#turning-it-off). Otherwise, while you are in an
instance belonging to one of your paired groups, only to that group's server:

- a request for the roster of the instance you are in, every 20 seconds and again after each
  join or leave, which tells the server the instance id;
- one live connection for that instance, which carries who joins and leaves and the flagged user
  card. It is a WebSocket; when the socket cannot be opened or keeps dropping, the companion asks
  for the same events with ordinary requests instead, and tries the socket again later. See
  [live updates](/api/live-updates) for the rules and the timings.
- a [heads-up](/companion/heads-ups), only when you press **Place** or **Clear** on one;
- one request for a person's card, only when you tap their row. It names that person.

The first two carry the client's token for that server, the instance you are in, and nothing else.
The person's card also goes out while you are in an [instance outside any group](#outside-group-instances),
to one paired server, carrying the token and the person and nothing about the instance. Nothing
the overlay does is sent to Modbot Cloud, and SteamVR is only handed the picture to draw.

The client window's **Servers** page shows the connection in one word next to each server:
**Off** (not in one of its instances), **Connecting**, **Live**, **Polling** or **Stopped**.
