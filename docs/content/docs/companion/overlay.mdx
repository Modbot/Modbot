---
title: The SteamVR overlay
description: The headset panel the companion draws for SteamVR, what it shows, what it needs, and its limits today.
---

The companion includes a SteamVR overlay: a panel meant to show a moderator, inside the
headset, who is in the group instance they are standing in and whether someone with a history of
kicks or bans has just walked in. Inside VRChat you cannot see email, Discord or a browser, so this
panel is how Modbot reaches you there.

The overlay is part of the same client you [install](/companion/install) and
[pair](/companion/pairing). There is nothing extra to download and nothing to switch on. If you do
not want it, [turn it off](#turning-it-off).

## What it needs

- **SteamVR**, or on Linux **WiVRn or Monado** (see [below](#wivrn-and-monado)). The companion
  never starts any of them itself. It looks for a running one every ten seconds and attaches the
  panel when it finds one; when the runtime closes, the panel lets go and comes back the next time
  it is started. Start them in either order.
- **A paired server** for the group whose instance you are in.
- **You in one of that group's instances.** Anywhere else, the panel shows its idle screen and no
  server is contacted.

Without SteamVR, or without a headset, the client simply runs without an overlay and keeps
reporting presence as normal. The overlay never stops the rest of the client from working: if it
cannot start, the client carries on without it.

The client window's **SteamVR** page shows where the overlay stands: attached (and to what),
not running, not installed, or refused (with the runtime's reason), when it attached, how many
frames it has drawn, what it is showing right now, and the panel's size and position. To see the
panel's picture on your desktop without a headset, start the client in
[debug mode](/companion/settings#debug-mode).

### WiVRn and Monado

On Linux with WiVRn or Monado, the companion draws the same panel through OpenXR instead of
SteamVR. It needs the OpenXR loader (`libopenxr_loader.so.1`) and Vulkan, both of which a machine
running WiVRn or Monado already has; there is nothing to install. xrizer alone is not enough,
because it runs OpenVR games only and turns overlay programs away, so the companion goes to the
OpenXR runtime itself. When the panel is up, the **SteamVR** page reads **Attached to WiVRn.** (or
the name of the runtime it found).

## Turning it off

The **SteamVR** page has an **Overlay on** switch at the top. Turn it off and the main panel
stops: nothing is drawn, your controllers are not read, and SteamVR is let go. The sidebar reads
**off** beside it.

The placement settings stay on the page, so you can arrange where the panel will sit before you
turn it on. What goes away is only what has nothing to report while it is off: how long it has
been attached, how many frames it has drawn, and what it is showing.

There are three panels and each has its own switch: this one, the
[notification overlay](/companion/settings#notification-overlay), and the
[desktop overlay](/companion/settings#desktop-overlay). None of them can take another down. The
live connection to your group's server is held open while any of the three is on and closes when
the last one goes off.

The rest of the client is unaffected either way: it goes on reading VRChat's log and reporting
presence to every server you paired with.

Turn it back on and the panel comes up again where you left it, without restarting the client.
The setting is remembered in `settings.json` as `overlayOn`, in your own user folder, so it
survives both restarts and updates.

## What it shows

### The roster

A list of who is in the instance right now, as your group's server knows it. At the top it names
the server the list comes from (its address, as shown on your **Servers** page), and how fresh the
list is.

People are sorted so the rows that matter come first:

| Dot | Who |
|---|---|
| Red | Flagged: someone your group has kicked or banned before. The row shows the count, such as `2 prior actions`. |
| Green | A member of the group. |
| Grey | Everyone else. |

The drawing code also has a place for staff, sorted after flagged people, but the server does not
mark anyone as staff yet.

If there is nobody to list, it says **Nobody here.** If no list has been loaded for this instance
yet, it says **No roster loaded for this instance.**

### Flagged user card

When someone your group has kicked or banned before joins the instance you are in, a card appears
above the roster: **Flagged user joined**, followed by the server's address, then the person's
display name and a line such as `1 prior moderation action`.

The server sends this card to the other paired moderators in that instance, not to the moderator
whose client reported the join first.

### Problem banner

A yellow banner at the top appears only when reporting has really stopped:

- *… rejected this device. Reporting and lookups have stopped; if you were removed from that
  group's staff, this is expected.* See [When a token is revoked](/companion/pairing#when-a-token-is-revoked).
- *Cannot reach …. Showing what was last known.* The server could not be reached and the roster is
  no longer fresh.

### Save a clip

With [Clips](/companion/clips) switched on, a wide control sits under the tabs: **Save a clip**.
Pressing it keeps the last few minutes of VRChat as a video file on your own PC. When nothing is
being kept it says why instead — *Waiting for VRChat*, *The folder cannot be used* — and cannot be
pressed; after a clip is written it says **Clip saved** for a few seconds. With Clips switched off
there is no control at all.

### Idle screen

When you are not in a group instance that one of your paired servers manages (including public,
friends-only and private instances), the panel shows nothing at all, and the client contacts no
server for it. The **idle** sample on the [debug page](/companion/settings#debug-mode) draws a
card reading **Not in a group instance** in the panel's place, so you can see where it sits.

**Save a clip** is the one thing still shown there, when Clips is on, because the companion records
wherever VRChat is running and a moment worth keeping can happen in a public instance too.

## Holding it

The panel can be moved with a controller:

| Do this | And |
|---|---|
| Point at the panel | A ring shows where you are pointing. |
| Hold the grip while pointing | The panel comes with your hand. Let go and it stays where you left it in the room, or on your hand if you let go with your hand at the panel. |
| Thumbstick up or down while holding | Pushes the panel away or pulls it closer. |
| Thumbstick left or right while holding | Makes it narrower or wider. |
| Grip twice quickly on the panel | Puts it back in front of your head. |
| Pull the trigger on the panel | Taps what you are pointing at: the alert card to dismiss it, a person's row to open their card, the card to close it, **Save a clip** to keep the last few minutes. |
| Thumbstick up or down over the list | Scrolls the roster. |

Where you leave the panel, its size, opacity and curve are saved and used next time. The
**SteamVR** page has the same settings, and **Put it back in front of me**.

On WiVRn and Monado the same works through OpenXR. The Valve Index, Oculus and Meta Touch, and
HTC Vive controllers are bound: grip holds, trigger taps, and the thumbstick or trackpad scrolls
and resizes. Any other controller gets OpenXR's simple controller bindings, where the select
button taps and the menu button holds, and there is no scrolling.

## How it follows you

The overlay uses the same reading of VRChat's log as the rest of the client, so it knows which
instance you are in without asking anyone. If you are paired with several groups, it shows only the
server whose group owns the instance you are standing in. Walking into another instance clears any
card about the one you left.

If VRChat's log stops growing for about two minutes, the client treats you as no longer in that
instance and the panel goes back to idle.

## How fresh it is

The panel always says how old its list is:

| It says | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **up to date** | Loaded in the last 45 seconds. |
| **as of N seconds ago** / **as of N minutes ago** / **as of N hours ago** | Older than that. Shown in yellow. |
| **not loaded** | Nothing has been loaded. |

The panel is drawn from what the client already holds, never from a request made while drawing. If
the server is slow or unreachable, you see the last list with its age, rather than a blank panel.
That list is kept in memory only, never written to disk, and is thrown away when you unpair the
server or quit the client.

## Limits

- **One card at a time.** A flagged user card stays up for 25 seconds, then clears itself.
- **The same person raises at most one card every five minutes**, so someone rejoining again and
  again does not flood the panel.
- **Only cards for your current instance.** A flagged join in a different instance is dropped, not
  saved for later.
- **The roster follows the instance.** While you are in a group instance the companion keeps a
  [live connection](/api/live-updates) to that group's server; a join or a leave re-reads the
  roster at once, and it is re-read every 20 seconds regardless.
- **Nothing on the panel moderates.** The trigger dismisses a card, opens a person's card, closes
  it again, and — if you have [Clips](/companion/clips) switched on — saves a clip to your own PC.
  That is all: there are no ban, kick or warn buttons, and the client's token could not do those
  things anyway. To act on something, use the web app.
- **The panel starts 0.45 metres wide** in the headset and is drawn at 1024 by 1024 pixels.
- **Long names are cut to one line**, so a display name full of line breaks cannot push the rest of
  the panel out of view.
- **SteamVR, WiVRn or Monado, on Windows or Linux.** The panel is an OpenVR overlay for SteamVR
  and an OpenXR overlay session for the other two; VRChat and the runtime run on the same machine
  as the companion. Other OpenXR runtimes, SteamVR's own included, have no overlay extension, so
  the companion has nothing to hand them.
- **It starts below and to the right of your view**, about an arm's length away, following your
  head. Move it with a controller or from the **SteamVR** page; see
  [Holding it](#holding-it).

## What it sends

Nothing at all while the overlay is [switched off](#turning-it-off). Otherwise, only while you are
in an instance belonging to one of your paired groups, and only to that group's server:

- a request for the roster of the instance you are in, every 20 seconds and again after each
  join or leave, which tells the server the instance id;
- one live connection for that instance, which carries who joins and leaves and the flagged user
  card. It is a WebSocket; when the socket cannot be opened or keeps dropping, the companion asks
  for the same events with ordinary requests instead, and tries the socket again later. See
  [live updates](/api/live-updates) for the rules and the timings.

Both carry the client's token for that server, the instance you are in, and nothing else. Nothing
the overlay does is sent to Modbot Cloud, and SteamVR is only handed the picture to draw.

The client window's **Servers** page shows the connection in one word next to each server:
**Off** (not in one of its instances), **Connecting**, **Live**, **Polling** or **Stopped**.
