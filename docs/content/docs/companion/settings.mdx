The client's **Settings** page has the start-with-Windows switch, the **Tell me about** card, the
**Voice** card, the [**Clips**](/companion/clips) card, the [**Listening**](/companion/listening)
card and the VRChat log folder. Everything else is set in the client's settings file or in environment variables on
your PC: the pairing page, update checks, Modbot Cloud backup, and how much the client writes to
its own log.

Nothing on this page is sent to any server. These settings decide what the client does on your PC.

## Voice

**Off by default.** With **Voice on**, the client says out loud what it sees, so a moderator in a
headset hears it rather than having to look at the overlay. The speech is made on your PC by a
small open-source text-to-speech engine that runs inside the client; nothing you hear, and nothing
about who joined, is sent anywhere to make it.

What it says is the **Voice** column of [Tell me about](#tell-me-about):

| Kind | What is said | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Joined** | *Rin joined your world*. Several people arriving together become *Rin and Kai joined your world*, or *five people joined your world*. | The client's own reading of VRChat's log, the same events the **Events** page shows. |
| **Already there** | *Rin was already in your world when you arrived*, folded together the same way. | The same. |
| **Left** | *Rin left your world*, folded together the same way. | The same. |
| **Changed avatar** | *Rin switched to the avatar “Tall Cat”*. | The same. |
| **Flagged join** | *Flagged user Rin joined*. | Your group's server, when it raises the same alert the [overlay](/companion/overlay) shows as a card. Only for the instance you are in. |
| **Log stopped** | *VRChat's log stopped while you were in this world*. | The client, when VRChat stops writing and it can no longer see who is there. |
| **Problem** | *Cat Lounge rejected this device. Modbot has stopped reporting to it.* | The client, when a paired server rejects its token and reporting has stopped. |

A fresh client says joins, leaves, flagged joins and problems, and keeps quiet about the rest.

It never announces you. It says nothing while reporting to any server is paused, and drops
anything it did not get to within a few seconds rather than reading out old news. Lines never
play over each other.

**Volume** is 0 to 100. **Test** speaks one line.

**Voice** is who speaks: **Bella**, **Nicole**, **Sarah**, **Sky**, **Adam**, **Michael**,
**Emma**, **Isabella**, **George** or **Lewis**. All ten come in the one download, so changing
your mind costs nothing and fetches nothing. A fresh client speaks as Bella.

**Output device** lists every output on your PC. **Windows default** (or **System default** on
Linux) follows whatever your operating system currently uses, and moves with it when that
changes, the way a voice chat program does. Pick a device and the client remembers it by its id;
if it is not plugged in when a line is due, the line plays on the default instead, the client's
log says so, and the device is used again as soon as it is back.

### The one download

The first time you turn the voice on or press **Test**, the client downloads the voice, once,
from the sherpa-onnx project's release on GitHub:

| | |
|---|---|
| Voice | Kokoro 82M, version 0.19, English: ten voices in one file, Apache-2.0 |
| From | `https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/releases/download/tts-models/kokoro-en-v0_19.tar.bz2` |
| Size | 319,625,534 bytes to download (about 305 MB); about 353 MB unpacked |
| SHA-256 | `912804855a04745fa77a30be545b3f9a5d15c4d66db00b88cbcd4921df605ac7` |
| Kept in | `%APPDATA%\Modbot\voices` |

The request carries nothing: no token, no id, nothing about you or your pairings. GitHub sees your
IP address, as it does for any download. A file whose size or SHA-256 does not match the one above
is thrown away, and the Voice card says so. The voice is not part of the installer, so a client
that never has the voice turned on never fetches it. The card shows the download's progress and
the size it is working through, and then **Voice ready**.

Clients before 2026-09-18 spoke with a smaller voice, `en_US-kristin-medium` from the Piper
project, which did not sound good enough. A client that has it fetches the new one the next time
the voice is turned on or **Test** is pressed, and deletes the old one once the new one is in
place — not before, so a download that fails leaves your PC as it was. While that is happening the
card says **Getting the new voice**.

On a PC with no sound output, or a Linux PC without OpenAL's library, the card says so and the
voice cannot speak; everything else in the client is unaffected.

The voice settings are kept in the settings file's `voice` object, listed
[below](#settings-in-the-settings-file).

## Modbot Cloud backup

**On by default.** The client sends a copy of its presence events to Modbot Cloud, for **every
instance you are in**. Separately, each group's Modbot still gets only its own group's instances, at
the address you paired with. The two never mix.

This does **not** send VRChat's log. It sends the events the client builds from the log, the same
ones described in
[What it sends to your group's Modbot](/companion/install#what-it-sends-to-your-groups-modbot):

- someone joined, was already there when you arrived, left, or changed avatar, and VRChat's log
  stopping while you were in an instance;
- for each: a random event id, the time, the person's VRChat user id and display name, the world
  id and instance id, the avatar's name for an avatar change, and the group that owns the instance.

The differences from what a Modbot server gets:

- It covers public, friends-only and private instances too. For an instance that belongs to no
  group, the group is left empty.
- It works the same whether the client is paired with no server, one, or several. A paired server
  has no say in where it goes or whether it is sent.
- **Pausing a paired server does not pause it.** Pausing stops the client reporting to that
  server; the backup is a separate flow and keeps going until it is turned off here.
- **The client's window does not show it.** The **Events** page says what happened to each event's
  own group's record — sent, waiting, withheld or failed — and nothing about the backup: no line
  of its own, no state, no filter, and no error if a batch of it fails. What the backup did is in
  the client's log file and in `%APPDATA%\Modbot\sent.jsonl`.

It never sends VRChat's raw log, the secret part of an instance's address that would let someone
join it, your pairing tokens, the client's own log, your computer's name or your Windows account
name.

Each batch also carries a batch id, the client's version, your PC's clock time, and how far your
clock is from Cloud's.

### Turning it off, or sending it somewhere else

Only the person using the PC can change this, in one of two places:

- In `%APPDATA%\Modbot\settings.json`, the `cloud` field:

  ```json
  {
    "cloud": {
      "endpoint": "https://cloud.example.org",
      "disabled": true
    }
  }
  ```

  Both parts are optional.

- In Windows environment variables for your account:

  | Variable | What it does |
  |---|---|
  | `MODBOT_CLOUD_ENDPOINT` | The Modbot Cloud to send to, instead of `https://cloud.modbot.co`. |
  | `MODBOT_CLOUD_DISABLED` | `1`, `true`, `yes` or `on` turns the backup off. `0`, `false`, `no` or `off` turns it on. |

Which one wins, for the address and for on or off separately:

1. The environment variable, if it is set.
2. `settings.json`, if it has the field.
3. The default: on, sending to `https://cloud.modbot.co`.

Things to know:

- The address must be a full HTTPS address. Plain HTTP is only accepted to your own PC
  (`localhost`, `127.0.0.1` or `::1`), for testing. Any other address is ignored and
  `https://cloud.modbot.co` is used instead. The client's log says when that happens.
- A value of `MODBOT_CLOUD_DISABLED` that is not one of the words above is ignored, and
  `settings.json` decides.
- The client reads these when it starts. Quit it from the tray and open it again after changing
  them.
- A client that starts with the backup off sends nothing, keeps nothing, and deletes anything left
  waiting from before. Turning it on again sends only what happens from then on.

### How Cloud knows your client

The first time the client sends to a Cloud address, it registers with it, sending only its version
and the word `windows`. Cloud gives back a random install id and a secret. The id is not your name,
your VRChat account or your PC. The client keeps both in `%APPDATA%\Modbot\cloud-installs.json`,
with the secret encrypted so only your Windows account can read it, and sends them with every
batch. If Cloud stops recognising the install, the client registers again.

### Batches, queue and retries

- Events are gathered into batches. A batch closes at 500 events, 256 KB, or 60 seconds after its
  first event, whichever comes first, and is sent compressed. A quiet instance sends nothing.
- While Cloud cannot be reached, events wait: up to 10,000 in memory, then on disk in
  `%APPDATA%\Modbot\cloud`, up to 20 MB. When either fills, the oldest are dropped.
- After a failure the client waits before trying again, a little longer each time up to five
  minutes, or as long as Cloud asks.
- A batch Cloud refuses as broken or too large is deleted, not retried.
- The client only asks Cloud's time when it has something to send, at most every two hours.
- Cloud backup runs on its own, so a slow or missing Cloud never holds up reporting to your group's
  server or the overlay.

Modbot Cloud keeps events for 365 days by default. See [Privacy](/privacy).

## Start Modbot Companion when my computer starts

**On by default**, and only shown in a copy installed with `Modbot-win-Setup.exe`. The portable
zip, and a copy you built yourself, do not show this switch — the **Settings** card it sits in is
not there at all — and never touch the Windows startup list.

While it is on, Modbot adds itself to your own Windows startup list and starts in the tray, with no
window, when you sign in to Windows. The client checks the entry every time it starts and fixes it
if needed, so it keeps working after an update.

Turning it off removes the entry. The choice is saved in the settings file as `startWithWindows`.

If you turn Modbot off in **Task Manager → Startup apps**, Modbot respects that: it never turns
itself back on, and the switch shows off and cannot be changed from the client. Turn it back on in
Task Manager if you want it again.

Uninstalling the client removes the startup entry.

## Settings in the settings file

The client keeps its settings in `%APPDATA%\Modbot\settings.json`. Every field is optional. The
switches and the Voice card write their own fields here, and the rest can only be set by editing
the file:

| Field | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `pairingPage` | `https://my.modbot.co/go?redir=/pair` | The page **Pair with a server** opens. See [Pairing page](#pairing-page). |
| `checkForUpdates` | `true` | Whether an installed client looks for newer versions. See [Check for updates](#check-for-updates). |
| `startWithWindows` | `true` | The **Start Modbot Companion when my computer starts** switch. |
| `vrchatLogFolder` | blank | The [VRChat log folder](#vrchat-log-folder). |
| `overlayOn` | `true` | The **Overlay on** switch on the **SteamVR** page. `false` stops the main headset panel entirely. See [Turning it off](/companion/overlay#turning-it-off). |
| `notifyOverlay.on` | `true` | Whether the notification overlay is drawn. See [Notification overlay](#notification-overlay). |
| `notifyOverlay.spot` | `topright` | Where on the screen it sits: `topleft`, `topmiddle`, `topright`, `bottomleft`, `bottommiddle` or `bottomright`. |
| `notifyOverlay.across` | `0` | Fine adjustment left or right, in metres, -1 to 1. |
| `notifyOverlay.down` | `0` | Fine adjustment up or down, in metres, -1 to 1. |
| `notifyOverlay.distance` | `1.0` | How far ahead of your head it sits, in metres, 0.3 to 3. |
| `notifyOverlay.width` | `0.35` | How wide, in metres, 0.1 to 1. |
| `notifyOverlay.opacity` | `0.95` | 0.1 to 1. |
| `notifyOverlay.seconds` | `6` | How long one pop-up stays, 2 to 30. |
| `desktopOverlay.on` | `false` | Whether the window over VRChat is available. See [Desktop overlay](#desktop-overlay). |
| `desktopOverlay.shortcut` | `mod+alt+m` | The keys that bring it up, `mod` meaning Ctrl. A combination the client cannot ask Windows for falls back to the default. |
| `desktopOverlay.opacity` | `90` | 20 to 100. |
| `desktopNotifyOverlay.on` | `true` on a new install, `false` if you already had a settings file | Whether notifications appear in a corner of your screen. See [Notification overlay on a monitor](#notification-overlay-on-a-monitor). |
| `desktopNotifyOverlay.spot` | `bottomright` | Which corner: `topleft`, `topmiddle`, `topright`, `bottomleft`, `bottommiddle` or `bottomright`. |
| `desktopNotifyOverlay.seconds` | `6` | How long one notification stays, 2 to 30. |
| `notifications.bleep` | `true` | Whether a short sound plays when you are told something. See [Notification sound](#notification-sound). |
| `notifications.volume` | `70` | The sound's own volume, 0 to 100, separate from the voice's. |
| `notifications.trayNoticesShown` | `0` | How many times you have been told the client is still in the tray. It stops after three. |
| `notifications.sound` | absent | A `.wav` file of your own to play instead of all five of Modbot's sounds. Absent means Modbot's own. See [Notification sound](#notification-sound). |
| `notificationFilters.popUp` | `["flagged join", "problem"]` | Which kinds raise a notification, in the headset and on a monitor alike. See [Tell me about](#tell-me-about). |
| `notificationFilters.sound` | `["flagged join", "problem"]` | Which kinds play the sound. |
| `notificationFilters.voice` | `["joined", "left", "flagged join", "problem"]` | Which kinds the voice says. |
| `voice.on` | `false` | The **Voice on** switch. See [Voice](#voice). |
| `voice.joins` | `true` | Kept in step with `notificationFilters.voice`, which is what the voice reads. Clients before 2026-09-19 used this. |
| `voice.leaves` | `true` | The same, for `left`. |
| `voice.flaggedJoins` | `true` | The same, for `flagged join`. |
| `voice.volume` | `80` | **Volume**, 0 to 100. |
| `voice.outputDevice` | absent | The chosen **Output device**, by the id the operating system gives it. Absent means the system default. |
| `voice.name` | `Bella` | Which of the download's ten voices speaks. A name that is not one of them falls back to Bella. |
| `cloud.endpoint` | `https://cloud.modbot.co` | Where Modbot Cloud backup goes. `MODBOT_CLOUD_ENDPOINT` wins over it. See [Modbot Cloud backup](#turning-it-off-or-sending-it-somewhere-else). |
| `cloud.disabled` | `false` | `true` turns Modbot Cloud backup off. `MODBOT_CLOUD_DISABLED` wins over it. |
| `clips.on` | `false` | The **Keep the last few minutes, with VRChat's sound** switch. Off until you turn it on; nothing is recorded while it is off. See [Clips](/companion/clips). |
| `clips.minutes` | `3` | How many minutes are kept, 2 to 5. Anything else is brought inside that range. |
| `clips.discordSound` | `false` | The **Discord's sound too** box. VRChat's sound has no setting of its own — it is what Clips means. Nothing else this PC plays can be recorded, and there is no setting for it. See [the sound](/companion/clips#the-sound). |
| `clips.folder` | absent | Where saved clips are written. Absent means your Videos folder plus `Modbot Clips`. |
| `clips.keepGigabytes` | `5` | How much room saved clips may take before the oldest are deleted, 1 to 200. There is no control for this on the screen. |
| `listenForPhrase.on` | `false` | The **Listen for "Modbot"** switch. Off until you turn it on; no microphone is opened while it is off. With it on, saying the name and then **clip that**, **show overlay** or **hide overlay** does those things. See [Listening](/companion/listening). |
| `listenForPhrase.microphone` | absent | Which microphone to listen on, by Windows' own id for it. Absent means the Windows default, followed wherever Windows moves it. Pick it on the **Listening** card rather than typing it here. |

For example:

```json
{
  "pairingPage": "https://modbot.example.org/pair",
  "checkForUpdates": false
}
```

Quit the client from the tray and open it again after editing the file.

Things to know:

- If the file is missing, or cannot be read as JSON, **every** setting in it uses its default. That
  includes Cloud backup and starting with Windows being on. The environment variables still apply.
- When you flip a switch, the client changes only that switch's field and leaves the rest of the
  file as it was; a change on the Voice card rewrites the whole `voice` object, and the same goes
  for `notifyOverlay`, `desktopOverlay`, `desktopNotifyOverlay` and `notifications`. It never
  writes the `cloud` field.
- If the file is broken, the client does not overwrite it. A switch you flip then only lasts until
  the client restarts.

### Pairing page

Set `pairingPage` to your own server's pairing page, for example
`https://modbot.example.org/pair`, to make **Pair with a server** go there directly instead of
through my.modbot.co.

The address must be HTTPS. Plain HTTP is only accepted to your own PC (`localhost`, `127.0.0.1` or
`::1`), for testing. Any other address is ignored and the default is used. The **Servers** page
shows which address the button opens.

You never have to set this: you can always open your server's `/pair` page in the browser
yourself.

### Check for updates

Set `checkForUpdates` to `false` to stop the client asking for newer versions at all, for example
if your group keeps everyone on one version, or the PC must not contact anything it does not have
to. Update checks are described under [Updates](/companion/install#updates).

This is separate from [Modbot Cloud backup](#modbot-cloud-backup): turning the backup off leaves
update checks running, and this setting leaves the backup running.

## VRChat log folder

Blank means the usual folder: the Windows profile, or on Linux the Proton prefix inside whichever
Steam layout the machine has ([which ones](/companion/install#what-it-reads)). The folder the
companion is watching is shown under the box and on the **Log** page. Name a folder to watch that
one instead -- a Steam library on another drive, say. It takes effect straight away; a log already
in the new folder is treated as history and not reported, the same as at a fresh start. The field
in the settings file is `vrchatLogFolder`.

## Client log level

The client writes a detailed log of what it is doing to `%APPDATA%\Modbot\logs`. To make it write
less, set the Windows environment variable `MODBOT_COMPANION_LOG_LEVEL` for your account, then restart
the client:

The values, from most to least: `Verbose`, `Debug`, `Information`, `Warning`, `Error`, `Fatal`.
Each writes that level and everything more serious. The default, `Verbose`, writes everything,
including a line each time the client reads new lines from VRChat's log. An unknown value means
`Verbose`. The log never leaves your PC unless you send it to someone.

`CONSOLE_LOG_MODE` changes the shape of what the client prints to a terminal it was started from —
`serilog`, `json` or `railway_json`, described in
[Console log modes](/self-hosting/environment-variables#console-log-modes). The log files are always
text.

## Overlay placement

`overlayOn` is the switch, and it is a field of its own so that moving the panel never rewrites
it. The placement can be set with the panel switched off, so you can arrange it before you turn
it on.

Where the main headset panel sits, set on the **SteamVR** page or by [holding it](/companion/overlay#holding-it)
with a controller, and saved as the `overlay` object in `settings.json`:

```json
"overlay": {
  "anchor": "head",
  "offset": { "x": 0.35, "y": -0.28, "z": -1.0, "qx": 0, "qy": 0, "qz": 0, "qw": 1 },
  "width": 0.45,
  "opacity": 1,
  "curve": 0
}
```

| Field | Means | Range |
|---|---|---|
| `anchor` | What the panel is fixed to: `head`, `lefthand` (your left wrist), `righthand` (your right wrist) or `world` (left in the room) | one of those four |
| `offset` | Where it sits from that anchor: metres as `x`, `y`, `z`, and a turn as `qx`, `qy`, `qz`, `qw` | any |
| `width` | How wide it is in the headset | 0.12 to 1.5 m |
| `opacity` | How solid it is | 0.1 to 1 |
| `curve` | How far it wraps around you | 0 to 1 |

A value outside its range is brought inside it when the file is read.

Choosing a wrist moves the panel to the [wrist position](/companion/overlay#on-your-wrist) and sets
`width` to 0.16, and leaving a wrist gives back 0.45 — unless you had chosen a width of your own,
which is kept.

## Notification overlay

A second headset panel, fixed in your view, where pop-ups appear: a flagged arrival, a Modbot
fault. It has its own switch and its own placement, and it is drawn whether or not the main panel
is. Nothing on it can be tapped.

`spot` names the corner or edge it sits in; `across` and `down` move it from there by a few
centimetres; `distance` is how far ahead of you it floats. `seconds` is how long one pop-up stays
before it fades. At most three pop-ups are drawn at once; older ones fall off the bottom.

```json
"notifyOverlay": {
  "on": true,
  "spot": "topright",
  "across": 0,
  "down": 0,
  "distance": 1.0,
  "width": 0.35,
  "opacity": 0.95,
  "seconds": 6
}
```

## Desktop overlay

The same roster, events and person card as the headset panel, in a window that sits over VRChat on
a monitor. Press the shortcut to bring it up and press it again to send it away, or use the
**Close** button on the window. Escape does not close it, because Escape is how VRChat opens its
own menu.

The shortcut is asked of Windows by name, so it works while VRChat has the keyboard. Another
program may already own the combination, and then the **Desktop overlay** card on the **Settings**
page says so and the **Open** button on that card is the way in.

Write the shortcut with `mod` for Ctrl, `alt` for Alt, then the key: `mod+alt+m`. Shift is not
written for a key that prints a character.

```json
"desktopOverlay": {
  "on": false,
  "shortcut": "mod+alt+m",
  "opacity": 90
}
```

VRChat in exclusive fullscreen will hide the window. Run VRChat in borderless windowed mode if you
want the overlay over it.

## Notification overlay on a monitor

Notifications in a corner of your screen, on top of whatever is there: a flagged arrival, a Modbot
fault, and anything else you have ticked in the **Pop-up** column of [Tell me about](#tell-me-about).
It has its own switch, and it is separate from the window above — the shortcut does not open it and
closing that window does not take it away.

`spot` is which corner it sits in, on the screen the Modbot window is on. `seconds` is how long one
notification stays. Nothing is drawn while there is nothing to say, and it never takes the keyboard
or the mouse from VRChat: a click where it is goes through to the game.

**It is on when you install Modbot.** If you were already running Modbot before 2026-09-19 it stays
off until you turn it on, because a window appearing over your screen after an update is not
something you asked for. The notification panel in the headset has been on from the start.

```json
"desktopNotifyOverlay": {
  "on": true,
  "spot": "bottomright",
  "seconds": 6
}
```

## Notification sound

A short sound when you are told something, with its own switch and its own volume, separate from
the voice. It plays through the same output device the voice uses. The same thing about the same
person does not sound twice within half a minute, and nothing sounds again until two seconds after
the last sound finished, so a rush of arrivals is one sound rather than ten.

```json
"notifications": {
  "bleep": true,
  "volume": 70,
  "trayNoticesShown": 0
}
```

`trayNoticesShown` counts how many times closing the window with the X has told you the client is
still running in the tray. After three it stops. Set it back to `0` to be told again.

### Which sound plays for what

There are five, and they are the same instrument saying different things: struck notes that come up
softly and decay with a tail, made by the client rather than read from a file, so there is no audio
file in the download whose origin anybody has to check.

| Sound | Plays when |
|---|---|
| **Chime** — one soft note | Somebody joined, left, was already there, or changed avatar, if you have ticked those in **Tell me about**. Also when a clip is saved because you said "Modbot, clip that" and the voice is off. |
| **Alert** — two notes, rising | Your group's server flagged somebody who has just arrived. |
| **Alert twice** — the same, twice | More than one different flagged person arrived within ten seconds. |
| **Urgent** — three notes, climbing | A paired server rejected this device so reporting has stopped, or VRChat's log stopped growing so the client can no longer see who is in the instance. |
| **All clear** — two quiet notes, falling | Nothing yet. It is built and you can hear it with the **All clear** button, but the client has nothing today that means "this is over". |

Nothing needs learning: the sounds get longer, higher and harder struck as the news gets worse. The
**Urgent** one is not louder than **Alert** — it is exactly the same height — it is brighter and
struck faster, which is what your ear reads as urgency.

A more serious sound is allowed to start inside the two-second gap a less serious one left behind
it, which is how two flagged people walking in together are heard as two. It only ever goes up, so
it cannot run away: a flood of flagged arrivals is about one sound every three seconds. Nothing ever
interrupts a sound that is already playing.

The sounds changed on 19 September 2026. Before that there was one, and before that it was two bare
sine tones, 880 then 1245 hertz, each switched on and off in six milliseconds — a smoke alarm rather
than a notification, and a moderator hearing it forty times an evening said so.

**Settings** → **Notifications** → **Test** has a button for each of the five.

### Using a sound of your own

**Settings** → **Notifications** → **Sound file**. Point it at a `.wav` file on your PC and that
plays instead — for all five, not one of them, so you lose the difference between them. Leave it
empty, or press **Use Modbot's sound**, to go back.

- **`.wav` only.** A WAV file is a header and the samples, which the client can read in sixty lines
  and no extra library. Any program on your machine will save one.
- **Plain WAV**, 8, 16, 24 or 32-bit, mono or stereo, at any sample rate. An MP3 renamed to `.wav`
  is refused rather than played as noise.
- **At most 10 seconds** is played, and files over 16 MB are refused.
- **A file that has gone missing, or that cannot be read, plays Modbot's own sound** and says why on
  the **Notifications** card. Press one of the **Test** buttons to see it.

The file is read once and then held, so forty sounds in an evening is one read. It is never copied
anywhere and no server is told it exists.

## Tell me about

The **Tell me about** card on the **Settings** page is one row per kind of event and one tick per
way of being told: a pop-up, the [notification sound](#notification-sound), and the
[voice](#voice).

**Pop-up** covers both notification overlays — the one in the headset and the
[one in a corner of your screen](#notification-overlay-on-a-monitor) — because they are the same
choice made once. Each has its own switch, so you can have either, both or neither.

| Kind | Pop-up | Sound | Voice | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Joined** | | | ✓ | Somebody joined the instance you are in. |
| **Already there** | | | | Somebody was already in the instance when you arrived. |
| **Left** | | | ✓ | Somebody left the instance you are in. |
| **Changed avatar** | | | | Somebody in the instance changed avatar. |
| **Flagged join** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | Your group's server raised a flagged-join alert for the instance you are in. |
| **Log stopped** | | | | VRChat stopped writing to its log, so the client can no longer see who is there. |
| **Problem** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | A paired server rejected this device, or cannot be reached. |

The ticks above are what a fresh client does. The first five kinds come from the client's own
reading of VRChat's log — the same events the **Events** page lists, under the same names.
**Flagged join** comes from your group's server, and **Problem** from the client itself.

Each way keeps its own switch and its own volume: turning a kind on here does nothing while the
sound is off, the voice is off, or both notification overlays are off. Your own arrivals and
departures are never announced, whatever is ticked.

The card writes the `notificationFilters` object:

```json
"notificationFilters": {
  "popUp": ["flagged join", "problem"],
  "sound": ["flagged join", "problem"],
  "voice": ["joined", "left", "flagged join", "problem"]
}
```

A word the client does not know is ignored rather than breaking the file.

Filtering on **who** arrived — everybody who is not age verified, say, or everybody who is 18+
verified — is not possible. Your group's server knows both, and shows them in the web app, but it
does not send either to the client, so the client cannot tell one arrival from another beyond
whether the server flagged it.

## Debug mode

Set the environment variable `MODBOT_DEBUG_MODE` to `1` for your account and restart the client,
and the window gains a **Debug** page:

- **Show overlay window** opens a window showing the overlay's last drawn frame: the same
  picture SteamVR is handed, so you can see the panel without a headset.
- **Look for SteamVR now** attaches to a running SteamVR at once.
- **Sample screens** pin a made-up screen into the overlay: **idle**, **roster**, **flagged
  join** or **problem**. **Live** lets the real screen back. Samples are built from constants in
  the client; nothing is fetched or sent for them.

Everything else runs as normal in debug mode, and the setting is read once, at start.
