title: Dictate
description: Speak a question to AI Chat from the headset panel or the desktop overlay. Every press opens a new chat of its own. Your words are written out on your PC, sent to AI Chat, and the answer shows in that chat. Your voice is never saved or sent.
---

In a headset you have no keyboard. **Dictate** lets you ask [AI Chat](/ai/chat) out loud instead of
typing: tap a button, say your question, tap again, and the answer appears in your headset. On a
monitor it works the same way from the [desktop overlay](#on-the-desktop).

## Using it

1. Tap **Dictate** on the small button panel, under **Hide** or **Show**, or on the headset
   [main panel](/companion/overlay#dictate), where it sits at the top. The button reads
   **Starting** while the microphone opens, then **Listening**, and a row of bars rises and falls
   with your voice. Words said while it reads **Starting** are kept.
2. Say your question.
3. Tap the button again. The microphone listens for a moment longer, so your last words are kept.
   The button reads **Writing** while your words are written out as text, then **Sending** while
   they go to AI Chat.
4. A new **Chat** opens as soon as your words are sent. It shows them with **Waiting for an
   answer** under them, and the answer appears there as it is written. The button goes back to
   **Dictate**.

**Every press of Dictate opens a new chat**, with a conversation of its own. Chats you opened
earlier stay where they are, and there is no limit to how many are open. To ask a follow-up in a
chat you already have, use that chat's own microphone or, on a monitor, its text box (see
[Chats](#chats)).

Each conversation is the same kind you see on the website's [Chat page](/ai/chat), so you can carry
on there with a keyboard, or read it again later.

If nothing was said, or nothing could be made out, nothing is sent and the button says **Nothing heard**
for a few seconds, then goes back to **Dictate**.

When something fails the button says so for a few seconds: **Could not listen**, **Could not write**,
**Could not get ready** or **Could not send**. A failed send also shows its reason in its chat.

## On the desktop

The [desktop overlay](/companion/settings#desktop-overlay) has a **Dictate** button in the strip along
its top, next to **Close**. It works the way the headset button does: click it, say your question,
click it again. The button shows the same words, **Starting**, **Listening**, **Writing**, **Sending**, and the same
bars. The answer shows in a new chat window.

You can also start and stop Dictate with a key, which is useful while VRChat has the keyboard. The
key is set in the app's **Settings**, on the **Desktop overlay** card, in the **Dictate** row: press
**Record** and then the keys you want. **Clear** takes it away again. There is none until you record
one, and it works while the desktop overlay is switched on. Press it once to start and again to stop,
just like the button.

## When the button is there

The **Dictate** button, on the small button panel, on the headset main panel and on the desktop overlay, only shows when all of these are true:

- AI Chat is switched on for your server ([Chat](/ai/chat#who-can-use-it)).
- You are allowed to use it: your role has **Use AI chat**
  ([roles and permissions](/accounts/roles-and-permissions#use-ai-chat)).
- Your Modbot account is linked to VRChat ([linking a VRChat account](/accounts/staff-accounts#linking-a-vrchat-account-required)).

If any of them stops being true, the button goes away, and so does the microphone in each open chat. The companion checks when it starts, every five
minutes, and when you pair it with a different server.

## Chats

Each chat is its own panel, in your headset or in a window on your monitor, and each has its own
microphone:

- In the headset a chat has a microphone and no text box. Tap its microphone, say your words, tap it
  again. To type, use the same chat's window on your monitor, which is there while the
  [desktop overlay](#on-the-desktop) is on.
- On a monitor a chat has a text box and a microphone. Type and press Enter, or use the microphone.
- A chat's microphone reads **Starting**, then **Listening**, in the same way as the main button.
- What you say or type in a chat goes into that chat's conversation and no other.

Each chat is held the same way as the other panels:

- **Lock** stops it being picked up, moved or resized.
- **Hand** makes it click-through, so pointing and clicking go to VRChat behind it.
- You can pick it up and move it, and drag its corner to make it bigger or smaller.
- The **X** closes that chat and ends its conversation. The others stay.

Every chat keeps its own Lock and Hand in the headset, and where you put it is remembered by its
number: the first chat opened is number 1, and when one is closed its number is used again by the
next. A chat opens where its number was last left. A number nobody has moved a chat in opens a step
to the side and down from the last, so chats do not sit on top of each other. Two quick grips send a
headset chat back to its own spot. See
[the bar under the panel](/companion/overlay#the-bar-under-the-panel) for what the buttons do.

On the desktop each chat is its own window. It opens on the screen VRChat is on, in front of it, and
never off the screen. Drag its top strip to move it and its bottom-right corner to resize it. The
**Lock** and the **Hand** are in the strip when **Edit mode** is on, the same switch as for the other
panels, and they work for all the chat windows together, as they do for the rest of the desktop
overlay. Its place and size are remembered by its number, like the headset's.

## Filing feedback

The overlay has no Feedback button. Ask a chat to file it, for example by saying "file feedback: the
instance list is missing the world name". The chat shows you a draft and files it only after you say
yes. See [Feedback](/companion/feedback#asking-chat-to-file-it).

## The first time

The first tap downloads a speech model, Moonshine base, of about 251 MB, once. A companion that
had the older, smaller model from before gets this one the same way, and the old one is deleted
when the new one is ready. The button reads **Getting ready** with
how much has come, as a percentage. Nothing you say is heard until it is done. When it is done the
button says **Ready**, then goes back to **Dictate**, and you tap it again to start speaking, so you
are never recorded the moment a download ends.

Dictate understands English only.

## What happens to your voice

- **Your voice is turned into text on your own PC.** It is not sent to a speech service.
- **The sound is never saved.** It is held only until your words are written out, then thrown away.
- **Only the written words are sent**, to AI Chat on your own server, the same as if you had typed
  them.
- The microphone is shared the way a voice chat program shares it, so VRChat and Discord keep working.
  It is the one you picked for [Listening](/companion/listening), or the Windows default.
- The microphone is only open between your two taps.

The model comes from GitHub, once, and nothing about you goes with the request. The
[privacy policy](/privacy) says the same.

## How long you can speak

A recording stops by itself after 60 seconds, as if you had tapped again.
