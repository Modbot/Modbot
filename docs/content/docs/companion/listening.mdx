title: Listening
description: Say "Modbot, clip that" and the companion saves a clip, so you never have to reach a keyboard in a headset. Off unless you switch it on, and nothing it hears is recorded, kept or sent.
---

You are in a headset, something happens, and **Save a clip** is on a panel you have to find and
point at. **Listening** is the answer to that: with it on, saying **"Modbot, clip that"** out loud
saves a clip, and the companion tells you whether it worked.

<Callout type="warn" title="This narrows something the companion used to promise">
Until 19 September 2026 the companion said, in its own source and on this site, that it **never**
recorded sound, by any route, and the build failed if any code that could open a microphone
appeared anywhere in it. That ban is now narrowed by exactly one file, and this page is the honest
replacement rather than a patch over it.

What has **not** changed: nothing your microphone hears is recorded, kept or sent — not to your
group's server, not to Modbot Cloud, not anywhere. There is no upload path in the client and it did
not gain one.

**Clips are not silent any more**, but that is nothing to do with your microphone: a clip carries
the sound VRChat is playing, and [that page](/companion/clips#the-sound) says what is and is not in
one. Your microphone is only ever opened by this switch, and only ever to hear one phrase.
</Callout>

## It is off until you switch it on

A fresh install has it off. An existing companion updated into a version that can listen has it off.
While it is off, **no microphone is opened** and nothing is asked of Windows' audio system at all —
there is no listener, no phrase matcher and no extra thread.

**Settings** → **Listening** → **Listen for "Modbot, clip that"** is the whole of the consent.

## What happens to the sound

Sound arrives from Windows in fifths of a second, is checked against four short phrases, and is
thrown away. That is the whole of it.

- **Nothing is written to disk.** Not a recording, not a transcript, not a log of what you said. The
  one file allowed to open a microphone cannot open a file for writing, and the build fails if it
  learns how.
- **Nothing is sent.** That file cannot reach the network either. The only thing listening ever
  sends is the one download of the phrase matcher itself, described below.
- **Nothing is held.** At most a few seconds of sound sit in memory waiting to be checked, and they
  are overwritten by the next few seconds.

### It is not transcribing you

The thing doing the checking is a **phrase matcher**, not a speech recogniser. It is a 3.3-megabyte
model that is handed four phrases and answers one question: *was one of those just said?* It has no
ability to produce a transcript of anything else, and the companion does not ship a recogniser that
could.

That is most of why it was chosen. The same library the companion already uses for the
[voice](/companion/settings#voice) also offers a full speech recogniser; a program that runs
unattended on your PC has no business carrying one.

## When the microphone is open

**Only while VRChat is running**, which is the same rule [Clips](/companion/clips) uses. The
companion knows VRChat is running because lines are arriving in VRChat's own log — it never looks
for a running program. VRChat closing closes the microphone. Quitting the companion closes it.

So the microphone is not open whenever the companion is. It is open while you are inside VRChat,
which is when the thing it exists for can happen.

**While it is open, the companion says so** at the top of every page of its own window, for as long
as it is open.

## It does not take your microphone away from VRChat

Windows is asked for the default microphone in **shared** mode — the same way VRChat, Discord and
every other voice chat program ask for it. Windows mixes them and hands each program the same sound.
Modbot never asks for the exclusive mode that would lock other programs out, and the build fails if
that call appears.

If some other program has already taken your microphone exclusively, Modbot cannot open it, the
**Listening** card says so, and everything else in the companion carries on exactly as before.

Modbot follows **whichever microphone Windows calls the default**. It never asks Windows for a list
of your microphones — the same as never asking for a list of your windows or your programs. Change
your microphone in Windows and Modbot changes with it.

## What you can say

Four spellings of the same request, because the matcher hears sounds rather than spellings and
"Modbot" is not a word it was trained on:

- **Modbot, clip that**
- **Mod bot, clip that**
- **Modbot, clip this**
- **Mod bot, clip this**

Say it normally. All four mean the same thing.

**The phrase cannot be changed.** The matcher is not given words — it is given the small pieces its
own vocabulary is built from, and working those out from a sentence needs a second library the
companion does not ship. The four above were worked out once, from the model's own vocabulary, and
written into the client.

### Saying it twice does not make two clips

One spoken sentence is one clip. After a phrase fires, nothing fires again for six seconds, which
covers the matcher offering the same words twice as the rest of the sentence arrives and covers you
repeating yourself because you were not sure it heard.

## What it says back

**If the [voice](/companion/settings#voice) is on**, it says it out loud:

| What happened | What it says |
|---|---|
| A clip was saved | *Clip saved.* |
| Clips is switched off | *Clips are switched off, so there was nothing to save.* |
| VRChat is not running | *VRChat is not running, so there was nothing to save.* |
| VRChat's window has not been found yet | *Modbot has not found VRChat's window yet, so there was nothing to save.* |
| The clips folder cannot be written to | *The clips folder cannot be used, so nothing was saved.* |
| The recorder had stopped | *Recording has stopped, so nothing was saved.* |

**If the voice is off**, a clip that was saved plays the
[notification sound](/companion/settings#notification-sound), and a clip that was **not** saved
plays nothing. One short sound cannot say which of six reasons it was, and a sound that meant both
"kept" and "not kept" would be worse than silence.

Either way it is written into the companion's **Events** page — including every time it heard you
and could not save anything — and the **Save a clip** control on the overlay says **Clip saved** or
**Clip not saved** for eight seconds, the same as when you press it yourself.

The answer arrives a moment after you speak, because the recorder writes the file on its own thread.
If nothing has happened after ten seconds it is called a failure, which is deliberately generous:
saying "not saved" about a clip that did land is the worse of the two mistakes.

## The one download

Turning **Listening** on fetches the phrase matcher once, from one pinned address:

```
https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/releases/download/kws-models/sherpa-onnx-kws-zipformer-gigaspeech-3.3M-2024-01-01.tar.bz2
17,626,723 bytes, SHA-256 f170013b4716e41b62b9bfd809687c207cef798ef9bc6534d524e17af9b6561a
```

About 17 MB, with nothing attached to the request: no token, no id, nothing about you or your
pairings. GitHub sees your IP address, as it does for any download. A download whose size or hash
does not match exactly is thrown away rather than used.

It lands in `%APPDATA%\Modbot\phrases`, and only the five files that are actually needed are
written: the three parts of the model, its vocabulary, and the list of phrases. The archive's spare
8-bit copies, its sample recordings and its readme are not written at all.

The model is Apache-2.0, from the sherpa-onnx project. It is listed in the repository's
`THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md` beside the voice.

## What it costs

Small, and deliberately so — your frame rate in VRChat matters more than how quickly a phrase is
noticed. The matcher is 3.3 million numbers, about a thousandth of the size of the voice, and it
runs on one thread below normal priority. Nothing here has been measured on a machine running
VRChat.

## On Linux

**Listening only works on Windows**, for the same reason [Clips](/companion/clips) does: saying
"Modbot, clip that" saves a clip, and there are no clips anywhere else. The Linux client says so on
the card rather than quietly doing nothing.

## Turning it off

Untick it. The microphone closes at once rather than at the next restart. Or set it in the
[settings file](/companion/settings#settings-in-the-settings-file):

```json
"listenForPhrase": { "on": false }
```

Deleting the `phrases` folder under `%APPDATA%\Modbot` removes the downloaded matcher as well; it
is fetched again the next time you turn listening on.
