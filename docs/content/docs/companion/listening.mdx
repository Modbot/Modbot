---
title: Listening
description: Say "Modbot" and then what you want — clip that, show overlay, hide overlay — so you never have to reach a keyboard in a headset. Off unless you switch it on, and nothing it hears is recorded, kept or sent.
---

You are in a headset, something happens, and **Save a clip** is on a panel you have to find and
point at. **Listening** is the answer to that: with it on, you say the companion's name and then
what you want — **"Modbot, clip that"**, **"Modbot, show overlay"**, **"Modbot, hide overlay"** —
and it tells you whether it worked.

<Callout type="warn" title="This narrows something the companion used to promise">
Until 19 September 2026 the companion said, in its own source and on this site, that it **never**
recorded sound, by any route, and the build failed if any code that could open a microphone
appeared anywhere in it. That ban is now narrowed by exactly one file, and this page is the honest
replacement rather than a patch over it.

What has **not** changed: nothing your microphone hears is recorded, kept or sent — not to your
group's server, not to Modbot Cloud, not anywhere. Clips are still silent. There is no upload path
in the client and it did not gain one.
</Callout>

## It is off until you switch it on

A fresh install has it off. An existing companion updated into a version that can listen has it off.
While it is off, **no microphone is opened** and nothing is asked of Windows' audio system at all —
there is no listener, no phrase matcher and no extra thread.

**Settings** → **Listening** → **Listen for "Modbot"** is the whole of the consent.

## It listens for its name, and only then for what you want

Nothing is listened for but the companion's own name. Sound goes to a matcher that has been given
two spellings of **Modbot** and can answer nothing else at all.

Only once one of those has been heard is the matcher that knows the commands handed any sound —
and only for the **next five seconds**. "Clip that" said across a table, or "hide overlay" in the
middle of a sentence, reaches nothing, because there is nothing there for it to reach.

Saying **"Modbot"** and nothing else does nothing. It starts those five seconds; if you say nothing
else, they end and the companion goes back to listening for its name. Nobody's clip is saved and no
panel moves because somebody said the word.

While it is waiting, the companion says so — on the **Listening** card and at the top of every page
of its window — and stops saying it the moment it would stop acting on you.

<Callout type="info" title="Leave a beat after the name">
The companion cannot hear a word that was said before it started listening for one, because it
keeps no sound. So "Modbot" and then, after a beat, "show overlay" is more reliable than running the
two together. One name gets one command; say the name again for the next one.
</Callout>

## What happens to the sound

Sound arrives from Windows in fifths of a second, is checked against the name — and, in those five
seconds, against the commands — and is thrown away. That is the whole of it.

- **Nothing is written to disk.** Not a recording, not a transcript, not a log of what you said. The
  one file allowed to open a microphone cannot open a file for writing, and the build fails if it
  learns how.
- **Nothing is sent.** That file cannot reach the network either. The only thing listening ever
  sends is the one download of the phrase matcher itself, described below.
- **Nothing is held.** At most a few seconds of sound sit in memory waiting to be checked, and they
  are overwritten by the next few seconds.

### It is not transcribing you

The thing doing the checking is a **phrase matcher**, not a speech recogniser. It is a 3.3-megabyte
model that is handed a short list of phrases and answers one question: *was one of those just
said?* It has no ability to produce a transcript of anything else, and the companion does not ship a
recogniser that could.

That is most of why it was chosen. The same library the companion already uses for the
[voice](/companion/settings#voice) also offers a full speech recogniser; a program that runs
unattended on your PC has no business carrying one.

## When the microphone is open

**Only while VRChat is running**, which is the same rule [Clips](/companion/clips) uses. The
companion knows VRChat is running because lines are arriving in VRChat's own log — it never looks
for a running program. VRChat closing closes the microphone. Quitting the companion closes it.

So the microphone is not open whenever the companion is. It is open while you are inside VRChat,
which is when the thing it exists for can happen.

**While it is open, the companion says so** at the top of every page of its own window, for as long
as it is open.

## It does not take your microphone away from VRChat

Windows is asked for the microphone in **shared** mode — the same way VRChat, Discord and every
other voice chat program ask for it. Windows mixes them and hands each program the same sound.
Modbot never asks for the exclusive mode that would lock other programs out, and the build fails if
that call appears.

If some other program has already taken your microphone exclusively, Modbot cannot open it, the
**Listening** card says so, and everything else in the companion carries on exactly as before.

## Choosing which microphone

**Settings** → **Listening** → **Microphone** lists the microphones your PC has. Pick your headset
microphone when you are in VR and your desk microphone when you are not; change it whenever you
like and the open one closes and the new one opens straight away.

**Windows default** is the first entry and is what you get until you pick something else. It follows
Windows: switch a headset on and Modbot moves with it.

A microphone you picked that is not plugged in **falls back to the Windows default** rather than
going quiet, and the card says so. Your choice is kept, so the moment it is back it is used again.

<Callout type="warn" title="This narrows something else the companion used to promise">
Until this list existed, Modbot asked Windows to follow the default microphone and **never** asked
which microphones your PC had — the same as never asking for a list of your windows or your
programs. Choosing one means asking for that list, so the promise is narrowed rather than left
standing next to code that contradicts it.

It is narrowed in the same shape as everything else here. **One file** in the companion may ask,
and it is the same file that is allowed to open a microphone; the build fails if a second one
learns to. The list is only asked for **while Listening is switched on** — with the switch off,
nothing at all is asked of Windows' audio system, exactly as before. And what comes back is a name
and an id for each device. Nothing is opened and nothing is heard by asking.
</Callout>

## What you can say

**The name**, which is all it listens for until it hears one of them. Two spellings, because the
matcher hears sounds rather than spellings and "Modbot" is not a word it was trained on:

- **Modbot**
- **Mod bot**

**Then, within five seconds, one of these:**

| What you say | What it does |
|---|---|
| **clip that** / **clip this** | Saves a clip, the same as pressing **Save a clip** |
| **show overlay** | Puts the headset panel back in front of you |
| **hide overlay** | Takes the headset panel away |

So: *"Modbot, clip that"*, *"Modbot, show overlay"*, *"Modbot, hide overlay"*. Say it normally.
"Clip this" means what "clip that" means.

### Which overlay show and hide act on

**The panel in your headset** — the one in front of your face, which is what you mean when you say
it while wearing one.

It does **not** touch the [notification pop-ups](/companion/overlay): those come and go by
themselves and are how you are told a flagged person walked in, so hiding them would lose the thing
you most need. It does not touch the
[window over VRChat](/companion/settings#desktop-overlay) either — that one takes your
keyboard when it comes up, which is right when you pressed a key for it and wrong when you said a
word inside a headset, and it has its own shortcut.

"Hide overlay" is a curtain, not a switch. It does not change anything in your settings file, the
panel is not taken down and rebuilt, and "show overlay" brings it straight back — showing what is
happening now, not what was happening when it went away. If you want it gone for good, untick
**Overlay** on the Settings page.

If the overlay is switched off altogether, or your headset is not running, it says so rather than
pretending it did something.

### The phrases cannot be changed

The matcher is not given words — it is given the small pieces its own vocabulary is built from, and
working those out from a sentence needs a second library the companion does not ship. Every phrase
above was worked out once, using the model's own tokenizer, with every piece checked against the
model's vocabulary before it was written into the client.

### Saying it twice does not do it twice

One spoken sentence is one action, and there are two rules holding that:

- **One name, one command.** The five seconds end the moment a command is taken. Anything else has
  to start again with the name.
- **Nothing fires twice within six seconds**, whichever command it was, which covers the matcher
  offering the same words twice as the rest of your sentence arrives and covers you repeating
  yourself because you were not sure it heard. It also means *"Modbot, show overlay"* immediately
  followed by *"Modbot, hide overlay"* does the first only — leave a few seconds between them.

## What it says back

**If the [voice](/companion/settings#voice) is on**, it says it out loud:

| What happened | What it says |
|---|---|
| A clip was saved | *Clip saved.* |
| Clips is switched off | *Clips are switched off, so there was nothing to save.* |
| VRChat is not running | *VRChat is not running, so there was nothing to save.* |
| VRChat's window has not been found yet | *Modbot has not found VRChat's window yet, so there was nothing to save.* |
| The clips folder cannot be written to | *The clips folder cannot be used, so nothing was saved.* |
| The recorder had stopped | *Recording has stopped, so nothing was saved.* |
| The panel was put up | *Overlay shown.* |
| The panel was taken away | *Overlay hidden.* |
| The overlay is switched off | *The overlay is switched off, so there was nothing to show.* |
| The headset is not running | *The headset is not running, so there was nothing to show.* |

**If the voice is off**, something that worked plays the
[notification sound](/companion/settings#notification-sound), and something that did not plays
nothing. One short sound cannot say which reason it was, and a sound that meant both "done" and
"not done" would be worse than silence.

Either way it is written into the companion's **Events** page — including every time it heard you
and nothing could be done — and for a clip the **Save a clip** control on the overlay says **Clip
saved** or **Clip not saved** for eight seconds, the same as when you press it yourself.

A clip's answer arrives a moment after you speak, because the recorder writes the file on its own
thread. If nothing has happened after ten seconds it is called a failure, which is deliberately
generous: saying "not saved" about a clip that did land is the worse of the two mistakes. Showing
and hiding the panel happens there and then, so those answer immediately.

## The one download

Turning **Listening** on fetches the phrase matcher once, from one pinned address:

```
https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/releases/download/kws-models/sherpa-onnx-kws-zipformer-gigaspeech-3.3M-2024-01-01.tar.bz2
17,626,723 bytes, SHA-256 f170013b4716e41b62b9bfd809687c207cef798ef9bc6534d524e17af9b6561a
```

About 17 MB, with nothing attached to the request: no token, no id, nothing about you or your
pairings. GitHub sees your IP address, as it does for any download. A download whose size or hash
does not match exactly is thrown away rather than used.

It lands in `%APPDATA%\Modbot\phrases`, and only the six files that are actually needed are
written: the three parts of the model, its vocabulary, `name.txt`, and `commands.txt`. The archive's
spare 8-bit copies, its sample recordings and its readme are not written at all. Those last two are
plain text and you can open them: they are exactly what your PC listens for.

The model is Apache-2.0, from the sherpa-onnx project. It is listed in the repository's
`THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md` beside the voice.

## What it costs

Small, and deliberately so — your frame rate in VRChat matters more than how quickly a phrase is
noticed. The matcher is 3.3 million numbers, about a thousandth of the size of the voice, and it
runs on one thread below normal priority. Nothing here has been measured on a machine running
VRChat.

## On Linux

**Listening only works on Windows**, for the same reason [Clips](/companion/clips) does: the first
thing it was built for was saving a clip, and there are no clips anywhere else. The Linux client
says so on the card rather than quietly doing nothing.

## Turning it off

Untick it. The microphone closes at once rather than at the next restart. Or set it in the
[settings file](/companion/settings#settings-in-the-settings-file):

```json
"listenForPhrase": { "on": false }
```

The microphone you picked is kept in the same object, as Windows' own id for it. Leave it out for
the Windows default:

```json
"listenForPhrase": { "on": true, "microphone": "{0.0.1.00000000}.{…}" }
```

Deleting the `phrases` folder under `%APPDATA%\Modbot` removes the downloaded matcher as well; it
is fetched again the next time you turn listening on.
