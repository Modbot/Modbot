title: Clips
description: Keep the last few minutes of VRChat while it is running, and save them as a video file — from the settings page or from inside your headset. Off unless you switch it on.
---

Something happens in an instance and it is over before you could have started recording. **Clips**
is the answer to that: while it is on, the companion keeps the last few minutes of VRChat, and
**Save a clip** writes those minutes out as a video file on your PC.

<Callout type="warn" title="This reverses something the companion used to promise">
Until 19 September 2026 the companion said, in its own source and on this site, that it **never**
captured the screen, by any route, for any reason. That is no longer true, and this page is the
honest replacement rather than a patch over it. What has **not** changed is the other half of that
promise: nothing the companion records ever leaves your PC. There is no upload path in the client
and it did not gain one.

It records **VRChat's window**, not your screen. The section below says exactly what that does and
does not keep out.
</Callout>

## It is off until you switch it on

A fresh install has it off. An existing companion updated into a version that can record has it off.
While it is off there is no recorder at all — no recording, no video encoder, no extra thread, and
nothing written anywhere.

**Settings** → **Clips** → **Keep the last few minutes** is the whole of the consent.

## What is recorded

**VRChat's window. Not your screen.**

The companion asks Windows for VRChat's own window, finds where it is drawn, and records that
rectangle — shrunk to at most 1280 pixels wide, at **15 frames a second**. A 2560×1440 window
records at 1280×720; a 1920×1080 one at 960×540. It is deliberately modest: your frame rate in
VRChat matters more than how sharp the clip is.

### What can still end up in a clip

Being straight about this, because it is the one thing on this page where "records VRChat's
window" is not the whole answer.

The companion takes a copy of a picture Windows has already drawn, and cuts VRChat's rectangle out
of it. So **anything drawn on top of VRChat while you are in VRChat is inside that rectangle and is
in the clip**:

- A chat program's in-game overlay, and Steam's.
- A Windows notification that pops up over the game.
- A window you pinned to stay on top.
- Modbot's own panel, if you have the [window over VRChat](/companion/overlay) open.

**While you are working in another program, nothing new is recorded at all.** Alt-tab to Discord
and the clip holds the last picture of VRChat until you come back — it does not start recording
Discord. That is the important half: VRChat's default is a borderless window filling the whole
screen, so without this rule a clip would be your whole monitor every time you tabbed away, which
is exactly when your private messages are on it.

The cost of that rule is worth knowing too: if you spend the whole recording in another program,
the clip is a still picture. A dull clip, not somebody else's messages.

## What is not recorded

- **Nothing outside VRChat's window.** Not a second monitor, not a window beside the game, not the
  taskbar, and not whatever you have alt-tabbed to.
- **No sound, at all.** Not your microphone, not your speakers, not VRChat's voice chat. The
  companion has no ability to record sound and the build fails if any code that could appears in it.
  That has not changed and is not going to.
- **Not the keyboard, not the clipboard, not a list of the programs you are running or of their
  windows.** It asks Windows for one named window — VRChat's — and never for a list of what else is
  open.
- **Nothing read out of a folder.** Not your Pictures, not your Documents, not your Desktop, not
  VRChat's own screenshot folder. The companion still never opens an image it did not make.
- **Nothing at all while VRChat is not running.** See below.

## It records only while VRChat is running

The companion knows VRChat is running because lines are arriving in
[VRChat's own log](/companion/install#what-it-reads) — the same file it already reads. It never goes
looking for a running program; it cannot, and that ban has not moved.

So: VRChat closes, recording stops and what was kept is deleted. VRChat opens, recording starts
again. The **Clips** card says which of those is happening — *Off*, *Waiting for VRChat*,
*Waiting for VRChat's window*, or *Recording*.

Because a clip is VRChat's window, the companion also has to be handed that window before there is
anything to keep. That takes a second or two after VRChat starts, and the card says
*Waiting for VRChat's window* while it does.

If VRChat's window closes, is minimised or is moved to a monitor the companion is not recording,
the clip holds the last picture of it rather than ending — and if you resize VRChat, or change your
resolution, the clip keeps its size and the picture is fitted inside it. A clip is never left half
written.

If VRChat is running but the companion has stopped understanding its log, it keeps recording. Lines
are arriving, so you are in a world, and a client that needs updating should not also quietly lose
the recording you switched on.

## Saving a clip from inside VR

**Save a clip** is on the [overlay panel](/companion/overlay), under the tabs, above whichever
screen is showing. From the roster, from the events list, from a person's card — it is one press.
It is on the headset panel and on the window over VRChat alike, so it works whether you play in a
headset or on a monitor.

It is only there when **Clips** is switched on. Turn Clips on and the control appears; leave it off
and the panel is exactly as it was.

**It tells you what is actually happening** rather than looking pressable and doing nothing:

| What it says | What that means |
|---|---|
| **Save a clip** | The last few minutes are being kept. Press it. |
| *Waiting for VRChat* | VRChat is not running, so there is nothing to keep. |
| *Waiting for VRChat's window* | VRChat is starting. This clears itself in a second or two. |
| *The folder cannot be used* | Clips cannot be written where they are pointed. The **Clips** card on the Settings page says why. |
| *Not available on this machine* | Recording does not work here. See [Windows only](#windows-only). |
| *Recording stopped* | Something went wrong. The **Clips** card says what. |

Only the first of those can be pressed. Nothing happens if you press any of the others, and the
control is not drawn as though it could be.

**After you press it, it tells you how it went** — **Clip saved** or **Clip not saved**, for about
eight seconds — because with a headset on there is no other way to find out. The clip is in your
clips folder, waiting for you when you take the headset off.

There is **no keyboard shortcut for this**, on purpose. The companion asks Windows for exactly one
keyboard combination — the one that brings up the [window over VRChat](/companion/overlay) — and
that is part of how it can say it does not read your keyboard. A second one is not worth what it
would cost that claim.

## How many minutes

Between **2 and 5**, on the slider. Anything else in the settings file is brought inside that range,
so a typo cannot put an hour and a half of video on your disk.

A saved clip covers **at least half** the length you chose, and up to all of it. The companion keeps
two overlapping recordings and hands you the longer one; that is what removes the case where you
press Save a moment after a recording started and get nothing. Save twice within a minute and the
second clip is short, because the first save is what started it.

## Where clips are written

By default, your own **Videos** folder plus **Modbot Clips** — Windows is asked where that is, so a
profile on another drive or a Windows in another language lands in the right place.

Change it in **Settings** → **Clips** → **Folder**. Leave it blank for the usual place. A folder
that cannot be written to — a read-only drive, a USB stick that is not plugged in, a typo — is shown
as a message on that card. Nothing else in the companion stops: it carries on reading VRChat's log
and reporting to your servers exactly as before.

While it is recording, the two rolling recordings live in the companion's own folder,
`%APPDATA%\Modbot\clips`, never in your Videos folder. They are deleted as they are replaced, when
VRChat closes, and when you quit the companion.

## What stops the folder filling your disk

Video is large, so three things bound it:

1. **The rolling recordings are not clips.** There are only ever two, each replaced as it fills, and
   all of them deleted when recording stops. At five minutes they come to about 110 MB together.
2. **The clips folder has a limit** — **5 GB** by default. Before a clip is saved, the oldest clips
   are deleted until the folder plus the new one fits. Set it with `keepGigabytes` in
   [`settings.json`](/companion/settings); there is no control for it on the screen.
3. **The newest clip is never deleted to make room.** A limit smaller than one clip will not delete
   the thing you just saved.

Only `.mp4` files the companion wrote are ever counted or deleted. Your own videos sitting in the
same folder are not Modbot's to touch.

## What leaves your PC: nothing

**No clip, no frame of one, and not even the fact that a clip exists is sent anywhere.** Not to the
Modbot server you paired with. Not to Modbot Cloud. Not to us.

The companion has no way to upload a file and did not gain one for this. The token it holds after
pairing can submit what it observes and read what the overlay needs, and nothing else — it cannot
attach anything to anything.

So a clip is a file on your PC, and it stays one until you do something with it.

## Attaching a clip to a ban, a kick or a case

Exactly the way you attach anything else: open the case in Modbot's web app and
[choose the file](/moderation/evidence). `video/mp4` is already an accepted kind of evidence, so a
saved clip needs nothing special.

The companion names each clip after the moment it was saved and the instance you were in, so the
right one is easy to find, and it writes a line into the companion's own **Events** page saying a
clip was saved and that it is on this PC only.

<Callout title="The companion does not do this for you">
There is no "record automatically when you ban somebody". A ban or a kick taken in Modbot never
reaches the companion — the only things a server pushes to a client are who joined and who left —
and giving a background client a credential that could upload video into your group's evidence
store would make a stolen token far more dangerous than it is. Pressing **Save a clip** is the
deliberate act, and choosing the file in a browser is the other one.
</Callout>

## What it costs while you are playing

The companion records at a low frame rate and a small size, uses your graphics card's own video
encoder when it has one, and does the work on a thread below everything else. On a machine that can
run VRChat this should be a percent or two of your graphics card's encoder and about as much of one
processor core.

That is an estimate, not a measurement. If it costs you frames, switch it off; the switch takes
effect immediately and nothing else about the companion changes.

## Windows only

There is no recording on the Linux companion, and there is no plan to add one. The **Clips** card
says so rather than failing quietly, and everything else on Linux works exactly as it does on
Windows.

The reason is the video encoder, not the recording. Windows has one built in, which is what the
companion uses. On Linux the usual answer is to run `ffmpeg` as a separate program, and this client
**never starts another program** — that is one of the things it can point at when somebody asks why
it should be trusted on a personal machine, and it was not going to be given up for this. The other
route, bundling a video library of its own, means shipping and maintaining something large for a
feature a few people would use.

A half-working recorder that wrote files nobody could open would be worse than one honest sentence,
so it is one honest sentence.
