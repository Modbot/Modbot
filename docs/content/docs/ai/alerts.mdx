---
title: Unusual activity
description: Alerts when a figure Modbot watches runs far outside your own normal - a burst of joins or flags, a busy instance nobody is watching, a drop in active members.
---

Insights arrive on a schedule. An **alert** arrives when something happens: a burst of joins, a run
of flags, an instance filling up with nobody watching it, a week with far fewer active members than
usual.

An alert is figures, not prose. It says what the figure was, what normal looks like, which stretch
of time it covers, and where in Modbot to go and look. If your AI is on, it can also carry one
sentence from the model about those figures — but the alert goes out either way.

Like an insight, an alert never names anybody and never suggests an action. It counts things.

You set alerts up under **Settings** → **AI** → **Alerts**, which needs the **Change settings**
permission. Everything is off until you turn it on.

## What Modbot can watch

| Watcher | What it notices | Over |
|---|---|---|
| **People joining the group** | VRChat group joins | an hour |
| **People joining Discord** | Discord server joins | an hour |
| **New accounts joining** | of those joiners, the ones whose VRChat account is less than a month old | an hour |
| **AI flags** | flags raised by your [AutoMod rules](/moderation/automod) | an hour |
| **Moderation actions** | bans, removals, instance kicks, warnings, rejections, and Discord bans, kicks and timeouts | an hour |
| **People leaving** | group and Discord leaves | an hour |
| **Instances opening** | group instances being created | an hour |
| **An instance filling up** | an open instance far busier than your instances usually get | now |
| **Nobody watching a busy instance** | a busy open instance with no moderator's client in it | now |
| **Fewer active members** | the week just ended against the four weeks before | a week |

Group joins and Discord joins are watched separately, because they are different doors.

**Active member** here means the same as it does on the [Discord analytics page](/analytics#discord):
somebody who sent a message or spent time in voice.

## How Modbot decides something is unusual

Never against a fixed number. A group of fifty and a group of five thousand have nothing in common
except that each knows what its own Tuesday evening looks like.

Modbot compares the stretch just ended with the **matching earlier stretches** — the same hour of
the day on each of the last 14 days, or the four weeks before this one. **Normal** is the middle of
those, and the **spread** is how far they usually sat from the middle.

A spike has to clear three bars at once:

- it has to be big enough on its own — five joins, three flags — so a quiet group never alerts on
  two of anything;
- it has to be several spreads above normal, and how many is the sensitivity you chose;
- it has to be at least half again normal, so a big steady number does not alert on a wobble.

A drop works the other way: **Fewer active members** alerts when the week comes in below a share of
normal — half at **Low**, about two thirds at **Normal**, three quarters at **High**.

Modbot says nothing at all until it has enough history to know what normal is: five matching
stretches, or three weeks for the weekly watcher.

**Nobody watching a busy instance** is the one that is not a comparison. It fires on an instance at or above
your own normal busiest instance — twice that at **Low**, half again at **Normal**, level with it at
**High** — with no moderator's client in it.

## Sensitivity

Each watcher is **Off**, **Low**, **Normal** or **High**.

- **Low** is the quietest: it takes a big, obvious spike.
- **Normal** is the middle.
- **High** notices more, and will sometimes notice a busy evening.

If a watcher is saying too much, move it down one. If something happened and it said nothing, move
it up one.

## Quiet time

After an alert, that watcher stays quiet for the **quiet time** — 6 hours by default — so one busy
evening does not fill your channel.

The exception is when it gets **much worse**: twice as far from normal as the alert that was posted.
Then it speaks up again, because a burst that keeps growing is worth hearing about more than once.

## Where alerts appear

- **In Modbot**, as a card at the top of the **VRChat** analytics page and of **Health**, showing
  alerts from the last 24 hours. Each row has **Open**, which takes you to the thing it is about —
  the member list filtered to the people who joined in that hour, the Flags page, the Live page, and
  so on. Reading them needs **See analytics**.
- **In Discord**, if you pick an alerts channel. This can be a different, noisier channel from your
  insights channel. The bot needs to see the channel, send messages and embed links in it. The card
  is amber, and shows the figure, what normal is for that hour or day, the stretch of time, and a
  link to the page the alert came from.
- **Anywhere else**, through [event routes](/discord/event-channels): every alert also writes an
  **Unusual activity** event, which a route can send wherever you like.

**×** on a card hides it. The alert itself is kept, and you can still find it through the event log.

## Settings

The **Alerts** card:

- **Discord channel** — where alerts go. Leave it empty to keep them in Modbot only.
- **Quiet time** — how long a watcher stays quiet after an alert.
- **AI sentence** — whether the model writes one sentence on each alert. Turning it off does not
  turn alerts off.

The **What is watched** card has one control per watcher.

Alert sentences are real AI requests and count towards spend under **Insights** on
[Limits and spend](/ai/limits-and-spend). If AI is off, or that limit is reached, alerts still go
out — with their figures and no sentence.

## When the checks run

Every 15 minutes. An hour-long stretch ends on the quarter-hour, so an alert reaches you within
about 15 minutes of the thing it is about.

**Fewer active members** is checked once a day, because it counts whole days and cannot change
inside one.
