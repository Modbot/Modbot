title: Join requests
description: The Requests page — the people waiting to be let into your group, and approving or rejecting one.
---

If your VRChat group is set to **Request to join**, people who want in end up in a queue. The
**Requests** page shows that queue, with what Modbot already knows about each person beside their
name, and **Approve** and **Reject** on each row.

## Where the list comes from

Unlike Members and Bans, this list is **not** a copy Modbot keeps. Modbot asks VRChat for the queue
when you open the page, and again each time you press **Refresh**. What you see is the queue as it
was a moment ago.

That is on purpose. This is the one list in Modbot whose rows vanish because somebody else acted —
a moderator answering the same request in VRChat, or the person withdrawing it — and a stored copy
would be a page of buttons that do nothing.

It also means:

- **There is no "last synced" line**, because nothing syncs.
- **There is no total.** VRChat does not say how many are waiting. The page has **Previous** and
  **Next**, and Next is available whenever the page came back full.
- **If Modbot cannot reach VRChat, the page says so** rather than showing an empty queue. An empty
  queue and a queue Modbot could not read look the same, and only one of them means there is
  nothing to do.

## What each row shows

The person, the way every other page in Modbot shows them: their name, their picture, their
[trust rank](/moderation/profiles), and their id. Clicking the name opens their
[profile popup](/moderation/popups), so you can read their history before deciding.

**Asked** is when VRChat says they asked.

**History** is the one thing this page adds that VRChat's own queue cannot:

| What it says | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Banned** | Your group's ban list holds them right now |
| **Banned before** | Your group banned them once and the ban was lifted |
| **Was a member** | They have been in the group before and left, or were removed |
| — | Modbot has nothing on record about them |

Most rows show a dash. Somebody asking to join a group is usually somebody nobody has heard of, and
that is fine — the point of the column is that the exceptions do not look like everybody else.

## Approving and rejecting

**Approve** lets them in. **Reject** turns their request down; they can ask again.

Both open a confirmation naming the person. Reject also offers the group's
[ban reasons](/moderation/case-files) and a note — both optional, unless your group has switched on
requiring a reason in **Settings**.

Once VRChat answers, the row leaves the list.

**Nothing is recorded unless VRChat accepted it.** If VRChat refuses, the row stays and the
confirmation says what VRChat said. If VRChat rate limits it, nothing happened and nothing is
retried — pressing again sooner only makes the wait longer.

### "That request is no longer waiting"

You will see this when the person is no longer in the queue: another moderator answered it, in
Modbot or in VRChat, or the person withdrew it. Nothing went wrong. The row leaves the list.

It happens more often than you would think when two people work the queue at once, which is why it
is written out rather than shown as an error.

### Modbot never blocks somebody from asking again

VRChat lets you reject a request *and* block the person from ever asking again. Modbot only ever
rejects. Blocking is a bigger decision than turning down one request and there is no undo for it
from this page — if that is what you want, [ban them](/moderation/members-and-bans) instead, which
also writes a case file.

## Permissions

| Permission | What it opens |
|---|---|
| **See join requests** | The Requests page |
| **Answer join requests** | The Approve and Reject buttons |

Neither comes with the built-in Moderator or Viewer roles, so grant them on the
[Roles](/accounts/roles-and-permissions) page. Administrators have both.

## What it writes down

Every approve and reject that VRChat accepted is recorded in the
[audit log](/moderation/audit-log) and on the person's profile, naming the moderator who decided.

VRChat's own group audit log records these too, but it says *Modbot* answered the request, because
Modbot signs in as its own account. Modbot's record is the one that says who actually decided.

An answer that VRChat refused is recorded as **Action failed** — never as something that happened.
