title: Reviews and repeat offenders
description: The list of people acted on more than once, the Past actions block on a person, and the Reviews page that asks the team to look when a moderator's pattern seems unusual or a moderation rule flags somebody.
---

Modbot reads your group's audit log from VRChat, so it knows every kick, warn, ban and removal any
moderator has done in VRChat. Two features count what is in that record:

- **People acted on more than once**, on the Bans page and on each person's popup.
- **Reviews**, which ask the team to look when one moderator's recent actions seem unusual.

Neither does anything to anybody. They count, and they ask.

## What counts as an action

Both features count the same things done to a person:

- kicked from an instance;
- warned;
- banned;
- removed from the group;
- a join request turned away (rejected or blocked).

Unbans are shown but never counted as an action against somebody: lifting a ban is not another
strike. Invites, accepted join requests and role changes are not counted either.

A moderator acting on their own account is never counted.

You can change which of those kinds count — see [Changing the rules](#changing-the-rules).

### One decision counts once

Some single decisions are recorded more than once. Banning somebody who is in one of your group's
instances makes VRChat record a ban **and** an instance kick, a second apart; a ban pressed in
Modbot is recorded by Modbot and by VRChat.

Modbot counts that as **one action**, by the moderator who made it, against the person it was made
against. The heavier record is the one that counts, so a ban with a kick beside it counts as a ban
and the kick is not counted again. Both are still in the
[audit log](/moderation/audit-log#one-decision-several-entries), as one row that opens into both.

Two decisions stay two. A ban, an unban, and a second ban ten minutes later is two actions.

## People acted on more than once

On the **Bans** page, the **People acted on more than once** tab lists everyone who has been acted
on at least twice, by any moderator, most recent action first. Opening the Bans page needs
**See the audit log**; the list itself needs **See profiles**.

At the top, the rule that decides the status is written out, for example **Repeat: 3 or more
actions in the last 30 days.**, and when the counts were last rebuilt.

Switch between **Everyone** and **Repeat only**. Up to 200 people are listed.

| Column | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Person** | Click to open their popup. |
| **Status** | **Once**, **More than once** or **Repeat** (see below). |
| **Actions** | All actions against them, ever. |
| **Last 30 days** | Actions in the last 30 days. |
| **Kicks**, **Warns**, **Bans**, **Removed**, **Turned away** | The count of each kind. **Kicks** are kicks from an instance; **Removed** is removal from the group. |
| **Moderators** | How many different moderators acted on them. |
| **Last action** | What it was, who did it and how long ago. |

The statuses:

- **Once**: one action, ever.
- **More than once**: two or more, but not enough recently to count as repeat.
- **Repeat**: 3 or more actions in the last 30 days. The 3 is the default, and can be changed.

### Changing the rules

**Settings**, **Moderation**, **Repeat offenders**, for anyone with
[**Change settings**](/accounts/roles-and-permissions#change-settings).

- **Repeat offender threshold** — how many actions in the last 30 days make somebody a repeat
  offender. Between 2 and 100.
- **Repeat offender types** — which kinds of action count towards it. Every kind counts unless you
  turn one off. At least one has to stay on.

Turning a kind off does not hide it. Its column still shows what happened; it just does not count
towards the status, and the **Actions** column no longer includes it.

Pressing **Save** rebuilds every person's counts before it answers, so the Bans page matches the new
rule straight away.

VRChat's group audit log does not record instance mutes — instance kicks and instance warns are the
two it records — so there is no mute to separate from a kick yet.

### Past actions on a person's popup

The same numbers appear as a **Past actions** block on the **Overview** tab of a
[person popup](/moderation/popups#person-popup), for anyone with **See profiles**. For example:
*Acted on 4 times by 3 different moderators, 2 in the last 30 days.* followed by the last and first
action, a count of each kind, the status and its rule.

If nobody has ever acted on them it says **No actions recorded.**

### How fresh the counts are

The counts are rebuilt from Modbot's records **every 15 minutes**, and each screen says when that
last happened (**Counts rebuilt** and a time). Before the first run they say **Counts not built
yet.**

They only cover what Modbot has recorded, which starts when Modbot first read VRChat's audit log.
Nothing before that is counted.

## Reviews

The **Reviews** page is in the sidebar right after **Flags**. It needs the
[**Review tickets**](/accounts/roles-and-permissions#review-tickets) permission. The number beside **Reviews** is how
many are waiting. While anything is waiting, the browser tab's title starts with the open reviews
and open flags you can see added together, as **(3) Modbot**.

A review is a **question, not a finding**. Modbot never judges a moderator, never limits what they
can do, and never messages them. It writes a plain sentence with the numbers in it, and waits for a
person to look.

**Review tickets** is a separate permission from the moderation ones on purpose: the people being
reviewed should not be the people closing their own reviews.

### What opens a review

The same run that rebuilds the counts, every 15 minutes, looks for two patterns. The moderator in a
review is the VRChat account VRChat's audit log names as having done the action.

**Keeps acting on one person.** One moderator has acted on the same person 3 or more times in the
last 30 days, in at least 2 different places, and no other moderator has ever acted on that person.
A "place" is an instance when Modbot knows it, otherwise the world, otherwise the day. Two kicks in
one evening in one instance are one place.

If other moderators **have** acted on the same person, the bar is much higher: 8 or more. That is
what keeps the persistent troll everybody removes from looking like a grudge.

**Far more actions than the rest of the team.** One moderator did 10 or more actions in one day
(counted in UTC), and that is at least 4 times the larger of:

- what the next busiest moderator did that day, and
- the team's usual number of actions per moderator per day.

Comparing against the next busiest moderator stops a raid night, when everybody is busy, from
opening a review on whoever happened to be busiest. This check does not run at all until the team
has at least 7 days with recorded actions, because before that there is no "usual".

"Usual" is actions over the last 90 days, up to yesterday, divided by the days a moderator did
anything. Today is left out, so a busy day cannot raise the usual it is compared to.

The numbers in this section are the defaults. There is no screen for changing them yet.

**Flagged by a moderation rule.** A third kind does not come from detection at all: a
[flag](/moderation/flags) sent here, either by a rule set to open a review for each flag or by a
moderator pressing **Open a review** on one. The "moderator" in that review is the rule. It shows
the rule and version, what was checked, the language, the words or picture that matched and the
model's reason.

### Reading a review

Switch between **Waiting** (with a count) and **Closed**. At the top right: **Detection last ran**
and a time.

Each review shows:

- what kind it is (**Keeps acting on one person** or **Far more actions than the rest of the
  team**), the moderator, and the person when there is one; all click through to popups;
- when it opened, and when its numbers were last updated;
- the sentence, for example *Acted on the same person 4 times in the last 30 days, across 3
  different instances or days (3 instance kicks, 1 ban). No other moderator has acted on this
  person.*;
- the numbers behind it: **Actions**, **Of which**, **Different instances or days**, **Other
  moderators who acted on them**, **Looking back**, **Day (UTC)**, **Next busiest that day**,
  **Team's usual per moderator-day**, **Their usual**, and **Rule applied**;
- **Covers** and the dates, and a button such as **Show the 4 fact ids**, which lists the ids of
  the audit log entries behind the numbers.

If you disagree with the question, you can see exactly how it came to be asked.

### Closing a review

<Steps>

<Step>

### Find out what happened

Open the moderator and the person, read their **Activity**, and ask the moderator if you need to.

</Step>

<Step>

### Write what you concluded

In **What did you conclude?**, for example *"Asked her; a crasher kept coming back on new accounts."*
A note is required, up to 2,000 characters.

</Step>

<Step>

### Press Close review

The review moves to **Closed**, showing who closed it, when, and the note.

A review that came from a moderation flag has two buttons instead: **The rule was right** and
**The rule was wrong**. Wrong dismisses the flag, which stops that rule and term flagging that
person ever again; right confirms it. Both feed the rule's numbers under **Settings**,
**AutoMod**.

</Step>

</Steps>

The note and who closed it are kept with the review and recorded in the
[audit log](/moderation/audit-log). "This was looked at and found fine" is worth keeping as much as
the pattern was.

If somebody else closed the same review a moment before you, Modbot says so.

### What happens after

- **A closed review is never reopened by the same actions.** Only actions that happen after it was
  closed can open a new review about the same moderator and person.
- **While a review is open, its numbers keep updating.** The same pattern does not open a second
  review.

## What these features cannot see

- **Anything VRChat's audit log does not record.** Everything here comes from actions done in
  VRChat.
- **Why an action was taken.** Kicks and warns have no reason in VRChat's audit log. Bans can have a
  [case file](/moderation/case-files), but the checks do not read case file reasons yet.
